# Consciousness Confidence Score

## Canonical Definition

The 0–100 score measures the **degree of epistemic justification for inferring consciousness in a candidate system, given the available evidence**.

It does **not** measure:

- the amount of consciousness in a system
- intelligence
- complexity
- humanness
- cognitive ability
- moral worth

The score answers one question:

> **Given everything observable, how justified are we in inferring that subjective experience is present in this particular system?**

## Scale

- **0** = essentially no credible evidence supporting an inference of consciousness
- **25** = weak evidence; a non-conscious explanation is substantially better supported
- **50** = genuinely ambiguous; available evidence does not strongly favor either inference
- **75** = strong converging evidence supporting an inference of consciousness
- **95+** = extremely strong inference from multiple independent lines of evidence
- **100** = direct certainty; under this framework, direct certainty is available only for one's own present subjective experience and is not assignable to an externally observed candidate system

## Evidence Dimensions

Candidate evidence may include:

- integration
- persistence
- self/world modeling
- valence
- flexible cognition
- metacognition
- learning
- architecture or mechanism
- behavioral evidence

Evidence against an inference may include:

- a sufficient simple mechanistic explanation
- fixed response patterns
- lack of relevant persistence
- lack of valence
- lack of integration
- lack of any plausible consciousness-supporting mechanism

No single indicator is automatically decisive.

## Boundary

This is an **epistemic confidence score**, not an ontological measurement.

A higher score means the evidence more strongly justifies the inference that consciousness is present. It does not mean the system contains "more consciousness."

Likewise, a low score means the available evidence does not justify a strong consciousness inference. It does not by itself constitute absolute proof of non-consciousness.

The framework therefore converts the binary question:

> Is it conscious: yes or no?

into the evidence question:

> How strongly does the available evidence justify inferring consciousness in this candidate system?
