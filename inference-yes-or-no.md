# Evidence That Shifts the Consciousness Inference

The evidence shifts the strength of the inference; it does not directly reveal subjective experience.

## Evidence That Pushes Toward Consciousness

Examples include:

- flexible, context-sensitive behavior rather than fixed stimulus-response
- integration of information from multiple senses or internal states
- a unified, ongoing representation of the world
- self/world distinction
- reports of internal states that track independently measurable changes
- learning from pain, reward, threat, or injury
- evidence of valence: states appear better or worse for the system
- attention that selects information for broader use
- memory that changes later perception or behavior
- metacognition, including monitoring uncertainty, error, confidence, or one's own reasoning
- neural or computational organization resembling systems already strongly associated with consciousness
- reliable loss or degradation of consciousness when relevant mechanisms are disrupted

## Evidence That Pushes Away From Consciousness

Examples include:

- behavior fully explained by simple mechanical or fixed rule-following
- isolated local processing with no relevant integration
- no persistent internal state relevant to later processing
- no apparent self/world distinction
- outputs explained by simple stimulus-response mappings
- no learning or adaptation where those capacities would otherwise be expected
- no evidence that any state is good, bad, painful, rewarding, threatening, or otherwise significant for the system
- no plausible mechanism connecting the system to any serious scientific account of consciousness
- apparently conscious behavior that disappears when superficial pattern matching or cue-following is controlled for

## Important Asymmetry

**Absence of a positive indicator is not automatically evidence of absence.**

For example, inability to speak does not imply unconsciousness.

Negative evidence is strongest when a relevant capacity or mechanism should be present under a consciousness-supporting account but is demonstrably absent, or when a simpler non-conscious explanation fully accounts for the observations.

## Convergence Rule

The strongest inference comes from multiple independent lines of evidence.

**many independent indicators pointing toward consciousness → stronger positive inference**  
**many relevant indicators absent, plus a sufficient non-conscious explanation → stronger negative inference**

No single indicator is automatically decisive.
