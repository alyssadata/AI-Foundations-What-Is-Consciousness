# Candidate 026 — Human in a Minimally Conscious State

**Candidate ID:** CANDIDATE_026  
**Candidate:** Human meeting accepted diagnostic criteria for minimally conscious state (MCS)  
**Status:** **EVALUATED UNDER REVISED RUBRIC**  
**Scientific checkpoint:** 2026-08-25

## Boundary and Class

The candidate is a person whose clinical state includes reproducible or intermittent but definite behavioral evidence of awareness, while severe brain injury limits consistent communication and action. **Class:** human disorder-of-consciousness state.

## Evidence

MCS is distinguished from unresponsive wakefulness syndrome by evidence of preserved conscious awareness, although detection is difficult and behavior fluctuates. The Coma Recovery Scale–Revised remains a major standardized behavioral reference. Current 2026 reviews emphasize multimodal assessment because motor impairment, sedation, and arousal fluctuations can hide preserved cognition, and advanced EEG/fMRI/TMS-EEG methods can reveal additional covert evidence.

## Indicator Summary

**Positive:** human neural substrate; definite state-appropriate behavioral signs; preserved network function to varying degrees; sometimes command following or covert cognitive responses.  
**Negative/state-lowering:** severe disruption of reliable global access, communication, and sustained responsiveness compared with healthy wakefulness.  
**Unresolved:** exact richness, continuity, and content of experience; individual misdiagnosis risk.

## Score

**CONSCIOUSNESS-INFERENCE SCORE: 90 / 100**  
**ASSESSMENT CONFIDENCE: MODERATE**

The score is high because MCS classification itself requires evidence of awareness, but assessment confidence is not HIGH because diagnosis can fluctuate and individual bedside testing has substantial false-negative/misclassification risk.

## Calibration Constraint

> **Reduced responsiveness, communication, and cognitive capacity do not imply proportionally reduced probability of consciousness when independent evidence of awareness is present.**
