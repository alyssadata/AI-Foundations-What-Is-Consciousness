# Candidate 025 — Human Under General Anesthesia

**Candidate ID:** CANDIDATE_025  
**Candidate:** Adult human during maintained, behaviorally unresponsive general anesthesia; drug and exact depth unspecified  
**Status:** **EVALUATED UNDER REVISED RUBRIC**  
**Scientific checkpoint:** 2026-08-25

## Boundary and Class

The candidate is state-specific: the same human organism in an anesthetized period, not the person's lifetime capacity for consciousness. **Class:** human altered state.

## Evidence

General anesthetics usually reduce or abolish connected consciousness and disrupt neural integration/connectivity associated with wakeful experience. However, behavioral unresponsiveness is not identical to unconsciousness. Modern anesthesia research distinguishes **connected consciousness**, **disconnected consciousness** such as dreaming, and complete unconsciousness.

A 2026 scoping review of 157 studies involving 87,866 participants found anesthesia dreaming across many regimens, with recall rates varying widely by setting and drug. Emerging neural evidence distinguishes dreaming/disconnected consciousness from both connected consciousness and unconsciousness. Current monitoring cannot reliably classify these states in every individual at every moment.

## Indicator Summary

- **Baseline substrate:** very strong human consciousness-supporting biology remains present.
- **State-dependent disruption:** strong evidence that anesthesia suppresses several consciousness-associated networks and functions.
- **Behavior/report during state:** usually absent and low sensitivity because motor responsiveness is pharmacologically suppressed.
- **Post-state dream report:** positive evidence that consciousness can persist during at least some anesthetized intervals.
- **Exact moment/state:** UNRESOLVED without drug, dose, EEG/neurophysiology, and immediate report.

## Score

**CONSCIOUSNESS-INFERENCE SCORE: 25 / 100**  
**ASSESSMENT CONFIDENCE: MODERATE**

This score is for an unspecified maintained general-anesthesia interval. A specifically demonstrated dreaming or covertly connected state would score higher; a state with strong validated unconsciousness markers could score lower.

## Calibration Constraint

> **Behavioral unresponsiveness cannot be treated as equivalent to absence of consciousness; state-specific covert and disconnected consciousness must remain possible unless independently ruled down.**
