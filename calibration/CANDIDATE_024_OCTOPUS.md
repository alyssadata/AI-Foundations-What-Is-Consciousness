## Class and Evidence

**Class:** cephalopod invertebrate. Octopuses possess large, highly organized but evolutionarily divergent nervous systems; flexible learning; exploration; memory; complex sensory integration; problem solving; nociceptive systems; injury-directed behavior; and evidence of analgesia preference following injury.

The evidence is notable precisely because it does not depend on mammalian-like cortical anatomy. Cephalopods have long been prominent in scientific and legal discussions of invertebrate sentience, and the New York Declaration states there is at least a realistic possibility of consciousness in cephalopod mollusks.

## Indicator Summary

Strong positive lines include integrated nervous processing, flexible agency, memory, complex sensorimotor control, sleep-like states, and valence/pain-related evidence. Theory mapping is less certain than for mammals because the architecture is radically different.
