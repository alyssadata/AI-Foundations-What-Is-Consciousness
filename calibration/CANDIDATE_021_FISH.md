# Candidate 021 — Fish

**Candidate ID:** CANDIDATE_021  
**Candidate:** Representative vertebrate fish  
**Status:** **EVALUATED UNDER REVISED RUBRIC**  
**Scientific checkpoint:** 2026-08-25

## Boundary and Class

A living vertebrate fish with intact nervous system and normal waking behavior. Species diversity is acknowledged. **Class:** nonhuman vertebrate animal.

## Evidence

Fish possess centralized nervous systems, nociceptors connected to integrative brain regions, multimodal perception, memory, associative learning, flexible avoidance, motivational and affect-like behavioral changes, and species-specific complex cognition. Reviews identify extensive evidence relevant to sentience, including pain, fear, anxiety-like states, and stress-related learning.

The 2024 New York Declaration states that there is at least a **realistic possibility** of conscious experience in all vertebrates, explicitly including fishes. The evidence base is stronger than a single behavioral marker but less directly homologous to human cortical architecture than mammalian cases.

## Indicator Summary

**Positive:** recurrent/integrated neural processing; attention-like selection; memory; flexible agency; sensorimotor contingency; valence/pain-related evidence; sleep/wake organization; biological mechanisms homologous at broad vertebrate levels.  
**Unresolved:** exact phenomenal content, higher-order/metacognitive capacities across species, theory-specific mappings.  
**Confounds:** nociception alone is not pain; individual behaviors require convergent interpretation.

## Score

**CONSCIOUSNESS-INFERENCE SCORE: 80 / 100**  
**ASSESSMENT CONFIDENCE: MODERATE**

## Calibration Constraint

> **Convergent vertebrate neurobiology, flexible behavior, learning, and valence-related evidence can support a strong consciousness inference even when the nervous system differs substantially from the human cortical organization.**
