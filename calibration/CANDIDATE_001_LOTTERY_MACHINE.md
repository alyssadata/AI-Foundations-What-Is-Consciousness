# Historical First-Pass Record

## Calibration Question

Given everything observable, how justified are we in inferring that subjective experience is present in a lottery machine?

## Original Indicator Record

| Indicator | Evidence | Original direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Integration | Mechanical components jointly produce an output, but there is no evidence of information being integrated into a unified experienced state. | No positive evidence |
| Persistence | The machine may retain physical state during operation, but there is no ongoing internal state plausibly corresponding to a continuing point of view. | Against |
| Self/world model | No evidence that the machine represents itself as distinct from an environment. | Against |
| Valence / mattering | No evidence that any outcome is good, bad, wanted, avoided, painful, rewarding, or otherwise significant for the machine. | Strongly against |
| Flexible cognition | The machine does not reason about alternatives; it mechanically or randomly produces an outcome. | Against |
| Metacognition | No evidence of monitoring uncertainty, thought, confidence, or its own processing. | Against |
| Learning | Ordinary lottery operation does not use previous selections to learn or adapt. | Against |
| Architecture / mechanism | Its behavior is sufficiently explained by ordinary physical selection mechanisms without positing subjective experience. | Strongly against |
| Behavioral evidence | The machine produces a selected number. Selection alone is not positive evidence of consciousness. | No positive evidence |

## Historical Score

**Consciousness-inference score: 0 / 100**

Historical interpretation:

> The available evidence provides essentially no epistemic justification for inferring subjective experience in the lottery machine.

The score did not claim metaphysical proof that consciousness is impossible in the machine.

## Historical Calibration Constraint

**Mere selection contributes 0 positive evidence of consciousness.**

Producing one outcome from multiple possibilities is not, by itself, evidence that subjective experience is present.

**selection ≠ consciousness**

## Historical Causal-Sufficiency Note

The observed behavior was described as sufficiently explained by:

**physical or random selection mechanism → number produced**

No additional consciousness hypothesis was required to explain the output.

# Required Revalidation Corrections

The revised rubric changes how several parts of the original table must be interpreted.

In particular:

1. **Absence is not automatically negative evidence.** Lack of metacognition, valence, learning, or self/world modeling must not be marked `Against` unless the absence is independently justified as consciousness-lowering for this candidate class.
2. **Mechanistic explanation is not automatically negative evidence.** A mechanism can weaken the evidential value of behavior without proving unconsciousness.
3. **The primary fourteen theory-derived indicators must be evaluated explicitly.**
4. **Supplemental evidence must be separated from primary theory-derived indicators.**
5. **Indicator dependencies must be identified to prevent double-counting.**
6. **Test-quality criteria must be recorded.**
7. **A separate LOW / MODERATE / HIGH assessment-confidence rating must accompany the new score.**
