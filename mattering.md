# Mattering and Valence

## Definition

If something **matters to a system**, it has significance for that system: for example, good, bad, preferred, avoided, rewarding, threatening, painful, or beneficial.

This is closely related to **valence**.

## Distinction

**subjective** = experienced from a point of view  
**mattering** = having significance or value for that point of view  
**consciousness** = subjective experience is present at all

Mattering is therefore not identical to consciousness.

A conscious experience need not carry strong positive or negative valence. Conversely, a system can be designed to optimize rewards, avoid penalties, or preserve variables without that optimization by itself establishing that anything subjectively matters to the system.

## Evidential Role

Evidence that states are genuinely good, bad, painful, rewarding, threatening, or otherwise significant **for the system** may strengthen an inference of consciousness.

But valence is one evidential dimension, not a standalone proof condition.

## Boundary

**mattering may support an inference of consciousness; mattering is not the definition of consciousness.**
