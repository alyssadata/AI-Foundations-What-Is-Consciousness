# Executive Summary

This repository treats **phenomenal consciousness** as the target phenomenon:

> **Consciousness is the presence of subjective experience: there is something it is like to be the candidate system at that time.**

The central methodological problem is that subjective experience in another system is not directly observable from the outside. Observable behavior, cognition, report, attention, memory, metacognition, biological organization, computational architecture, and causal structure may provide evidence relevant to consciousness, but none is definitionally identical to subjective experience.

Accordingly, this project does not claim to directly detect consciousness. It asks a narrower epistemic question:

> **Given the available evidence, how justified are we in inferring that subjective experience is present in this candidate system?**

The current scientific literature does not provide one accepted universal consciousness test applicable across humans, animals, AI systems, and arbitrary physical objects. The most defensible contemporary strategy is therefore **convergent, theory-aware, population-specific inference** with explicit attention to test validity, evidential dependence, false positives, false negatives, and unresolved uncertainty.

The repository operationalizes that approach through a theory-derived indicator battery, supplemental cross-theory evidence, a test-quality gate, an evidential-status system, a 0–100 consciousness-inference score, and a separate assessment-confidence rating.

Two low-end calibration candidates have been completed under the revised rubric:

| Candidate | Consciousness-inference score | Assessment confidence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Ordinary mechanical lottery machine | **0 / 100** | **HIGH** |
| Ordinary passive blanket | **0 / 100** | **HIGH** |

These scores mean that the available evidence supplies essentially no epistemic justification for inferring subjective experience in those candidates. They are **not** metaphysical proofs that consciousness is impossible in them.

No AI candidate has yet been scored under the revised framework. Therefore this report does **not** establish a consciousness score for current LLMs or AI agents.

# 1. Target Construct

The working target is **phenomenal consciousness**.

The minimal condition is:

> **There is something it is like to be the entity at that moment.**

Possible conscious contents can include perception, pain, pleasure, warmth, cold, touch, emotion, imagery, inner thought, memory as experienced, effort, uncertainty, spatial awareness, temporal experience, or a sense of self or presence. No particular item in that list is required.

The repository therefore distinguishes consciousness from several neighboring capacities:

**selection ≠ consciousness**  
**cognition ≠ consciousness**  
**attention ≠ consciousness**  
**functional access ≠ phenomenal consciousness**  
**self-report ≠ proof of consciousness**  
**metacognition ≠ consciousness**  
**intelligence ≠ consciousness**  
**complexity ≠ consciousness**

These capacities can become evidence when an appropriate theory or validated test gives them evidential relevance. They are not substitutes for the target itself.

Full definition: [`consciousness-defined.md`](consciousness-defined.md).

# 2. Why Consciousness Must Be Inferred

For another system, subjective experience is not directly available to an external observer.

The inferential structure is therefore:

**observable evidence → theory/test interpretation → inference about subjective experience**

This makes the problem fundamentally epistemic rather than merely classificatory.

The framework does not ask whether a candidate can behave intelligently, answer questions, choose among alternatives, preserve state, report feelings, or react to its environment. It asks how much those observations actually justify the additional claim that experience is present.

A behavior can be real while its evidential value for consciousness is zero.

Example:

**lottery machine selects a number**  
→ selection occurred  
→ selection alone supplies no positive evidence of subjective experience

This distinction is preserved throughout the repository.

# 3. Current Scientific Position

## 3.1 No universal consciousness test

Current consciousness science does not provide a single accepted test that is simultaneously decisive and valid across all possible candidate systems.

Bayne, Seth, Massimini and colleagues' modern C-test framework emphasizes that proposed tests must be evaluated relative to:

- **target population**;
- **specificity** — how often a non-conscious system could pass;
- **sensitivity** — how often a conscious system could fail;
- **rational confidence** — how justified those estimates and interpretations are.

This prevents a measure developed for one population from being uncritically transferred to another.

## 3.2 Current AI-specific method

Butlin, Long, Bayne and colleagues provide the most current purpose-built theory-derived framework represented in this repository for assessing consciousness indicators in AI systems.

Its general structure is:

**scientific theories of consciousness → indicator properties → empirical system assessment → updated credence in consciousness**

The method does not treat indicators as a binary checklist. Indicators can overlap, depend on one another, differ in sensitivity and specificity, and provide different strengths of evidence.

## 3.3 Major theories remain unsettled

No single leading consciousness theory is treated here as established fact.

The 2025 COGITATE adversarial collaboration directly tested preregistered predictions from Integrated Information Theory and Global Neuronal Workspace Theory in human participants using multiple neuroimaging and electrophysiological methods. The results supported portions of each theoretical picture while substantially challenging important predictions from both.

The consequence for this repository is methodological:

> **No one theory automatically controls the score.**

Convergence across genuinely distinct theories, mechanisms, methods, and interventions is more informative than repeated evidence generated by the same underlying assumption.

## 3.4 Human clinical assessment is population-specific

Clinical consciousness assessment illustrates why one universal test is unlikely to be sufficient.

Relevant methods can include:

- standardized behavioral assessment;
- overt command following;
- covert command following using EEG or fMRI;
- resting-state and task-based neurophysiology;
- TMS-EEG and Perturbational Complexity Index approaches;
- lesion, anesthesia, stimulation, and recovery evidence.

These methods are useful because they are tied to human neurobiology and validated or studied within relevant populations. They are not automatically valid tests for software, stars, blankets, or arbitrary physical systems.

# 4. Revised Consciousness-Inference Rubric

The current rubric uses a **primary theory-derived indicator battery** plus supplemental evidence.

## Primary theory-derived indicators

### Recurrent Processing

- algorithmic recurrence;
- organized integrated perceptual representation.

### Global Workspace

- specialized parallel subsystems;
- limited-capacity workspace with selection;
- global availability / broadcast;
- state-dependent attentional control.

### Computational Higher-Order Accounts

- generative or top-down perceptual processing;
- metacognitive monitoring;
- metacognition-guided belief/action updating;
- structured quality-space coding.

### Attention Schema

- model of attention.

### Predictive Processing

- predictive coding.

### Agency and Embodiment

- minimal flexible agency;
- output-input contingency modeling.

## Supplemental evidence

The rubric separately records:

- temporal integration / continuity;
- memory-related organization;
- self/world modeling;
- valence / mattering;
- causal perturbation and complexity;
- biological / cellular substrate evidence;
- physical causal integration;
- behavioral / report evidence;
- arousal / wakefulness where biologically relevant.

These supplemental dimensions prevent the primary AI-oriented battery from being treated as exhaustive consciousness science.

Full rubric: [`RUBRIC.md`](RUBRIC.md).

# 5. Evidence Classification

Every important observation is classified as one of:

- **POSITIVE** — raises the consciousness inference under a credible account;
- **NEGATIVE** — lowers the inference for a defensible reason;
- **NEUTRAL / ABSENT** — no positive evidence is present, but the absence is not itself evidence against consciousness;
- **UNRESOLVED** — evidence is unavailable, disputed, inaccessible, or dependent on an unsettled system boundary;
- **NOT APPLICABLE** — the indicator or test is not meaningful for the candidate class;
- **CONFOUNDED** — apparent evidence is contaminated by imitation, cueing, surface mimicry, or an alternative mechanism.

This distinction corrects an important failure mode in simple consciousness checklists:

> **Missing positive evidence is not automatically negative evidence.**

For example, lack of language, valence, metacognition, or self/world modeling cannot automatically be treated as proof of unconsciousness.

---

# 6. Test-Quality Gate

Before evidence can materially alter a score, the framework asks whether the test itself deserves evidential weight.

The gate evaluates:

- target-population fit;
- specificity;
- sensitivity;
- rational confidence;
- construct validity;
- gameability / mimicry;
- independence from other indicators.

This is especially important when evaluating AI systems, where a system may produce highly consciousness-like language without necessarily implementing the mechanism that gives similar behavior evidential force in humans.

Likewise, ordinary physical properties cannot be promoted into consciousness indicators simply by renaming them with cognitive vocabulary.

# 7. Scoring

The framework reports two separate outputs.

## 7.1 Consciousness-Inference Score

The 0–100 score measures:

> **the degree of epistemic justification for inferring consciousness in the candidate system, given the available evidence.**

Current anchors:

- **0** — essentially no credible evidence supporting the inference;
- **25** — weak evidence; non-conscious accounts substantially better supported;
- **50** — genuinely ambiguous overall evidence;
- **75** — strong converging evidence;
- **95+** — extremely strong inference from multiple independent high-quality lines of evidence;
- **100** — direct certainty, reserved within this framework for one's own present subjective experience rather than an externally observed candidate.

The number is not calculated by assigning equal points to rubric rows. The indicators overlap too substantially for a scientifically justified flat sum.

The score is therefore currently a **structured epistemic confidence index**, not a validated probability.

## 7.2 Assessment Confidence

Every candidate also receives:

> **ASSESSMENT CONFIDENCE: LOW / MODERATE / HIGH**

This describes confidence in the **quality and completeness of the evidence used to assign the score**.

A low consciousness-inference score with high assessment confidence means something different from the same score based on poor or incomplete evidence.

Full scoring specification: [`confidence-score.md`](confidence-score.md).

# 8. Calibration Results

## Candidate 001 — Ordinary Mechanical Lottery Machine

**Consciousness-inference score:** **0 / 100**  
**Assessment confidence:** **HIGH**

The revised-rubric evaluation identified no credible positive consciousness indicator.

The candidate's salient behavior — selecting one outcome from multiple possibilities — is adequately characterized as ordinary physical randomization and selection. That explanation does not prove that mechanistic systems cannot be conscious; it removes the evidential force of the selection behavior itself.

### Preserved calibration constraint

> **Mere selection contributes 0 positive evidence of consciousness.**

### Additional calibration constraint

> **Ordinary physical persistence, component interaction, randomness, and state change must not be relabeled as consciousness-relevant information-processing indicators without evidence that the candidate actually implements the relevant mechanism.**

Full record: [`calibration/CANDIDATE_001_LOTTERY_MACHINE.md`](calibration/CANDIDATE_001_LOTTERY_MACHINE.md).

## Candidate 002 — Ordinary Passive Blanket

**Consciousness-inference score:** **0 / 100**  
**Assessment confidence:** **HIGH**

The blanket persists, changes temperature, deforms, absorbs moisture, and retains physical traces of earlier conditions. None of those facts currently provides credible positive evidence of subjective experience.

Importantly, the score does not arise from counting absent cognition as repeated negative evidence. Most consciousness-related indicators are classified as neutral/absent or not applicable.

### Preserved calibration constraint

> **A physical state that covaries with environmental conditions does not, by itself, constitute sensing, representation, awareness, or experience.**

### Additional boundary

> **Passive material response is not automatically functional responsiveness in a consciousness-relevant sense.**

Full record: [`calibration/CANDIDATE_002_BLANKET.md`](calibration/CANDIDATE_002_BLANKET.md).

# 9. What the Calibration Has Established So Far

The first two candidates establish a defensible low-end floor without requiring the framework to pretend that every absent cognitive capacity is evidence against consciousness.

So far, the calibration supports four reusable constraints:

1. **Selection alone provides no positive consciousness evidence.**
2. **Ordinary physical persistence and state change are not consciousness evidence merely because they can be described informationally.**
3. **Environmental covariation is not automatically sensing or representation.**
4. **Passive response is not automatically consciousness-relevant functional responsiveness.**

These constraints matter because later candidates will contain progressively stronger forms of sensing, feedback, memory, prediction, agency, representation, biological organization, and language.

The low-end anchors prevent those later properties from receiving evidential credit merely because weak physical analogues can be found in every object.

# 10. What Has Not Been Established

This project has **not** established:

- a universal detector of consciousness;
- necessary and sufficient conditions for phenomenal consciousness;
- a validated probability model for consciousness;
- scientifically justified equal weights for the rubric indicators;
- that absence of any one indicator proves unconsciousness;
- that mechanistic explanation is incompatible with consciousness;
- that biological substrate is either necessary or irrelevant;
- that Integrated Information Theory, Global Workspace Theory, Higher-Order Theory, Recurrent Processing Theory, Predictive Processing, Attention Schema Theory, or any other current theory is definitively correct;
- a consciousness score for current LLMs;
- a consciousness score for persistent-memory AI agents;
- scores for animals or humans beyond the future calibration queue.

Those questions remain open until their relevant candidates are evaluated under the same method.

# 12. Current Conclusion

The strongest conclusion currently justified is methodological rather than ontological:

> **Consciousness in another system should be treated as an inference from convergent evidence, not as something established merely by cognition, behavior, complexity, selection, report, physical persistence, or environmental response.**

The project therefore separates three questions that are often collapsed:

**What does the system do?**  
**What mechanisms does the system implement?**  
**How strongly do those observations justify inferring subjective experience?**

The first two questions are externally investigable. The third is the consciousness inference.

At the present calibration state, both evaluated low-end candidates score **0 / 100 with HIGH assessment confidence**, while the broader framework remains explicitly provisional and open to revision as candidate testing and consciousness science advance.
