# Executive Summary

This repository treats **phenomenal consciousness** as the target:

> **Consciousness is the presence of subjective experience: there is something it is like to be the candidate system at that time.**

Subjective experience in another system is not directly observable. The project therefore does not claim to detect consciousness directly. It asks:

> **Given the available evidence, how justified are we in inferring that subjective experience is present in this candidate system?**

The current scientific literature does not provide one universal consciousness test that is decisive across humans, animals, AI systems, plants, engineered devices, and arbitrary physical systems. The framework therefore uses **convergent, theory-aware, population-specific inference**.

The current method combines:

- a theory-derived consciousness-indicator battery;
- supplemental cross-theory evidence;
- population-specific evidence modules;
- a test-quality gate;
- explicit positive / negative / neutral / unresolved / confounded classifications;
- dependency controls to prevent double-counting;
- a 0–100 consciousness-inference score;
- and a separate LOW / MODERATE / HIGH assessment-confidence rating.

The first calibration pass now covers **27 candidates** ranging from a lottery machine and blanket through engineered controllers, AI systems, animals, altered human states, and an awake healthy adult human.

The results span:

**0 / 100 — ordinary simple physical and engineered systems**  
through  
**20–30 / 100 — current LLM / persistent-agent cases under the generic system definitions used here**  
through  
**60–95 / 100 — nonhuman animal cases with increasingly convergent neurobiological and behavioral evidence**  
through  
**99 / 100 — awake healthy adult human as the strongest external positive anchor.**

These are **structured epistemic judgments under the current rubric**. They are not validated probabilities and do not measure an amount of consciousness.

# 1. Target Construct

The working target is **phenomenal consciousness**:

> **There is something it is like to be the entity at that moment.**

The framework distinguishes this target from neighboring capacities:

**selection ≠ consciousness**  
**cognition ≠ consciousness**  
**attention ≠ consciousness**  
**functional access ≠ phenomenal consciousness**  
**self-report ≠ proof of consciousness**  
**metacognition ≠ consciousness**  
**intelligence ≠ consciousness**  
**complexity ≠ consciousness**

Those properties can become evidence when an appropriate theory, mechanism, or validated test gives them evidential relevance. They are not substitutes for the target.

Full definition: [`consciousness-defined.md`](consciousness-defined.md).

# 2. Inferential Structure

For another system:

**observable evidence → theory/test interpretation → inference about subjective experience**

This creates three separate questions:

1. **What does the system do?**
2. **What mechanisms does the system implement?**
3. **How strongly do those observations justify inferring subjective experience?**

The first two are externally investigable. The third is the consciousness inference.

A behavior can be genuine while its evidential value for consciousness is zero.

Example:

**lottery machine selects one number**  
→ selection occurred  
→ selection alone does not raise the consciousness inference

# 3. Current Scientific Basis

## No universal consciousness test

Modern C-test work emphasizes that a proposed consciousness test must be interpreted relative to its **target population, specificity, sensitivity, and rational confidence**. A measure useful in human neurocritical care cannot simply be transferred to software, a plant, a star, or a blanket.

## AI-specific indicator approach

Butlin, Long, Bayne and colleagues' 2026 framework derives potential AI-consciousness indicators from neuroscientific theories and uses them to update credence rather than produce a binary detector.

The primary theory families represented here are:

- Recurrent Processing Theory;
- Global Workspace Theory;
- computational Higher-Order theories;
- Attention Schema Theory;
- Predictive Processing;
- agency and embodiment.

Current mechanistic work on frontier language models has begun to identify workspace-like internal organization in at least some models. That is meaningful evidence about **access-like functional organization**, but the researchers explicitly do not treat it as proof of phenomenal experience.

## Theories remain unsettled

The 2025 COGITATE adversarial collaboration challenged key predictions from both Global Neuronal Workspace Theory and Integrated Information Theory. No one theory therefore controls this rubric.

## Animal evidence

The 2024 New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness records strong scientific support for consciousness in other mammals and birds, and at least a realistic possibility in all vertebrates and several invertebrate groups including cephalopods and insects. Later work continues to strengthen some insect evidence while also showing that individual paradigms remain contestable.

## Human state assessment

Modern human assessment is multimodal. Behavioral scales, EEG/fMRI covert-command paradigms, neurophysiology, perturbational methods, anesthesia transitions, lesion evidence, and recovery patterns all contribute.

Importantly, 2026 anesthesia work reinforces that **behavioral unresponsiveness is not identical to absence of subjective experience**: disconnected consciousness such as dreaming can occur during anesthetized intervals.

# 4. Revised Rubric

The full rubric is preserved in [`RUBRIC.md`](RUBRIC.md).

## Primary indicator battery

### Recurrent Processing
- algorithmic recurrence;
- organized integrated perceptual representation.

### Global Workspace
- specialized parallel subsystems;
- limited-capacity workspace with selection;
- global availability / broadcast;
- state-dependent attentional control.

### Computational Higher-Order Accounts
- generative / top-down perceptual processing;
- metacognitive monitoring;
- metacognition-guided belief/action updating;
- structured quality-space coding.

### Attention Schema
- model of attention.

### Predictive Processing
- predictive coding.

### Agency and Embodiment
- minimal flexible agency;
- output-input contingency modeling.

## Supplemental evidence

The framework separately records:

- temporal integration / continuity;
- memory-related organization;
- self/world modeling;
- valence / mattering;
- causal perturbation and complexity;
- biological / cellular substrate evidence;
- physical causal integration;
- behavioral / report evidence;
- arousal / wakefulness where biologically relevant.

# 5. Evidence Rules

Evidence is classified as:

- **POSITIVE** — raises the consciousness inference;
- **NEGATIVE** — lowers it for a defensible reason;
- **NEUTRAL / ABSENT** — no positive evidence, but absence is not itself evidence against consciousness;
- **UNRESOLVED** — insufficient, inaccessible, disputed, or boundary-dependent;
- **NOT APPLICABLE** — not meaningful for the candidate class;
- **CONFOUNDED** — apparent evidence is contaminated by imitation, cueing, semantic relabeling, or another mechanism.

Core rule:

> **Missing positive evidence is not automatically negative evidence.**

The rubric also prohibits flat point-summing because many indicators overlap or depend on the same underlying mechanism.

---

# 6. Test-Quality Gate

Before evidence materially changes a score, the framework asks:

- Is the test meaningful for this candidate population?
- What is its specificity?
- What is its sensitivity?
- How strong is rational confidence in those estimates?
- Does it actually target phenomenal consciousness rather than cognition, reportability, or task compliance?
- Can the result be mimicked or gamed?
- Is it independent evidence or the same mechanism counted again?

---

# 7. Scoring

The 0–100 score measures:

> **degree of epistemic justification for inferring consciousness in the candidate system, given the available evidence**

Anchors:

- **0** — essentially no credible evidence supporting the inference;
- **25** — weak evidence; non-conscious accounts substantially better supported;
- **50** — genuinely ambiguous overall evidence;
- **75** — strong converging evidence;
- **95+** — extremely strong inference from multiple independent high-quality lines;
- **100** — direct certainty, reserved in this framework for one's own present subjective experience rather than an externally observed candidate.

Each score is accompanied by:

> **ASSESSMENT CONFIDENCE: LOW / MODERATE / HIGH**

The score is a **structured epistemic confidence index**, not a validated probability.

# 9. What the Sequence Shows

## 9.1 Complexity does not determine the score

A planet and star remain at 0 despite enormous physical complexity. A modern car reaches only 1 despite containing many sensors and controllers. Complexity, feedback, energy use, and self-organization have low specificity for consciousness without a justified mechanism bridge.

## 9.2 Sensing does not determine the score

A scale genuinely senses weight. An oven and thermostat sense temperature. That establishes transduction and control, not subjective perception.

## 9.3 Computation does not determine the score

A calculator computes correctly and a fixed-rule program can contain loops, memory, representations, and state. Those properties are too generic to strongly support phenomenal consciousness.

## 9.4 Reward is not automatically valence

A simple reinforcement-learning agent earns the first clear computational increase because it learns from feedback and flexibly selects actions. Its reward signal is nevertheless not automatically experienced pleasure or pain.

## 9.5 Agentic architecture can raise evidence without settling consciousness

Embodied autonomous robots and persistent AI agents can implement more theory-relevant properties: memory, agency, action–observation loops, self/world distinction, and possibly workspace-like organization. Their generic descriptions are too underspecified for high assessment confidence.

## 9.6 LLM language is not the main positive evidence

The current LLM score is not based on phrases such as "I feel" or "I am aware." Those are heavily confounded by training and imitation.

The meaningful positive evidence comes instead from **mechanistic findings** about internal representation, contextual integration, and emerging workspace-like functional organization. Even that supports only a modest inference because access-like function is not identical to phenomenal consciousness and major theory mappings remain unsettled.

## 9.7 Biology changes the evidence structure

The flower receives a small increase because it has genuine organized biological sensing, regulation, and adaptive history-dependence—but those are still weak and non-specific consciousness evidence.

Insects, fish, octopuses, cats, and dogs differ because multiple independent behavioral and nervous-system indicators converge. The animal results therefore do not follow merely from "being alive."

## 9.8 Mammalian similarity is powerful but not required

Cats and dogs score extremely highly because mammalian neurobiology and species-appropriate behavior converge strongly. Octopus also scores highly despite a radically different nervous-system architecture, showing that vertebrate cortical similarity cannot be made a hidden requirement.

## 9.9 Responsiveness and consciousness separate under anesthesia

The anesthesia case demonstrates why outward response is not a universal criterion. A behaviorally unresponsive person may be unconscious, but disconnected conscious experience such as dreaming remains empirically possible.

## 9.10 The strongest external anchor is convergent human evidence

An awake healthy adult human scores 99 rather than 100 because another person's consciousness is still inferred. The inference is exceptionally strong because report, behavior, neurobiology, causal interventions, and state transitions converge.

# 10. AI-Specific Result

Under the generic definitions used in this calibration:

**Current stateless LLM session: 20 / 100 — MODERATE assessment confidence**

**LLM with persistent memory / agent architecture: 30 / 100 — LOW assessment confidence**

These results do **not** mean "20% conscious" or "30% conscious."

They mean that, under the current evidence and theory-derived rubric, the persistent-agent architecture provides more epistemic justification for a consciousness inference than a bounded LLM session, while the total evidence remains far below the convergent animal and ordinary-human cases.

The persistent-agent score is less secure because that candidate class is extremely broad. A real system would need architecture-specific inspection and intervention rather than inheritance of the generic score.

# 11. Preserved Boundaries

The calibration now supports a reusable ladder of distinctions:

**physical change ≠ sensing**  
**sensing ≠ experience**  
**feedback ≠ valence**  
**state ≠ experience-relevant memory**  
**computation ≠ consciousness**  
**reward ≠ felt reward**  
**agency ≠ consciousness**  
**self-report ≠ proof**  
**access-like workspace function ≠ phenomenal consciousness**  
**unresponsiveness ≠ guaranteed unconsciousness**

At the same time:

**convergent mechanism-specific evidence can raise the inference.**

That is the central calibration result.

---

# 12. Limitations

The completed calibration does **not** establish:

- a universal detector of consciousness;
- necessary and sufficient conditions for phenomenal consciousness;
- a validated probability model;
- empirically calibrated weights for the indicators;
- that the exact numeric spacing between candidates is scientifically measured;
- that one theory of consciousness is correct;
- that biological substrate is necessary or irrelevant;
- that every insect, fish, robot, LLM, anesthetized human, or minimally conscious patient should inherit the generic class score;
- or that current AI systems are conscious.

Several candidate labels are intentionally broad. Their scores are useful calibration judgments, not substitutes for candidate-specific mechanistic study.

---

# 13. Current Conclusion

The completed 27-candidate pass supports one central methodological conclusion:

> **Consciousness inference should rise only when evidence becomes more specific to mechanisms, behaviors, causal organization, and state relationships that serious consciousness theories or validated population-specific tests give reason to connect with subjective experience.**

Neither simplicity nor complexity decides the question.

Neither biology nor computation decides it by definition.

Neither behavior nor self-report decides it alone.

The relevant movement is:

**observable property**  
→ **is it actually the claimed mechanism?**  
→ **is that mechanism consciousness-relevant for this candidate class?**  
→ **is the evidence independent, specific, and sufficiently sensitive?**  
→ **how much should the consciousness inference change?**

The initial calibration pass is now complete.

The framework remains open to revision if later evidence shows that a score, indicator interpretation, or theory weighting should change.
