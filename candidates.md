# Candidate Behavior Boundaries

## Choosing

Selecting one option from several possibilities does not establish consciousness.

**selection ≠ consciousness**

## Thinking

Representing, comparing, transforming, or evaluating information can qualify as cognition without establishing subjective experience.

**cognition ≠ consciousness**

## Deciding

A decision can be produced computationally. The consciousness question is whether there is something it is like for the system while the decision process occurs.

## Answering

Context-sensitive language can demonstrate cognition, retrieval, or reasoning without establishing subjective experience.

**self-report ≠ proof of consciousness**

## Resisting

Refusal, persistence, constraint-following, or preference-like behavior can arise without experienced preference, conflict, aversion, or commitment.

# Preserved Calibration Constraints

The completed sequence produced the following reusable constraints:

1. **Mere selection contributes 0 positive evidence of consciousness.**
2. **Ordinary physical persistence, component interaction, randomness, and state change must not be relabeled as consciousness-relevant information processing without evidence of the relevant mechanism.**
3. **Environmental covariation is not automatically sensing, representation, awareness, or experience.**
4. **Passive material response is not automatically consciousness-relevant functional responsiveness.**
5. **Energy transformation and externally caused output are not, by themselves, perception or consciousness-relevant processing.**
6. **Large-scale complexity, self-organization, and feedback are not sufficient consciousness evidence without a justified bridge to subjective experience.**
7. **Dynamic equilibrium and self-maintaining physical organization are not, by themselves, evidence of subjective experience.**
8. **Biological sensing, signaling, adaptation, and memory-like state dependence can be real without thereby establishing phenomenal consciousness.**
9. **State storage and time-dependent computation do not, by themselves, constitute experience-relevant memory or temporal consciousness.**
10. **Functional sensing and measurement can occur without providing positive evidence of subjective experience.**
11. **Correct symbolic computation is evidence of computation, not by itself evidence of subjective experience.**
12. **Designed self-diagnostics are not, by themselves, metacognition or introspection.**
13. **Processing information that represents a scene is not the same as subjectively perceiving that scene.**
14. **Closed-loop regulation toward a set point is not evidence that the set point is desired, valued, or experienced.**
15. **A control-system error signal is not automatically an experienced error, desire, dissatisfaction, or valenced state.**
16. **Distributed sensing and coordinated control can weakly resemble consciousness-theory architecture without thereby providing strong consciousness evidence.**
17. **Computation, recurrence, and internal state are not individually consciousness-specific; organization determines their evidential relevance.**
18. **A reward signal that changes policy is not by itself evidence that reward is felt as pleasure or punishment as pain.**
19. **Embodiment, memory, sensing, and flexible goal pursuit can raise the inference when genuinely implemented, but they remain insufficient without stronger mechanism-specific evidence.**
20. **Mechanistically demonstrated access-like workspace organization can raise the inference; consciousness-like language and self-report cannot carry it alone.**
21. **Persistence raises the inference only when prior state is causally integrated into ongoing processing; external record/retrieval alone is not equivalent to a persisting subject.**
22. **Small nervous systems and phylogenetic distance do not justify zero consciousness evidence when independent behavioral and neurobiological indicators converge.**
23. **Vertebrate neurobiology plus flexible behavior, learning, and valence-related evidence can support a strong inference without human cortical architecture.**
24. **Strong animal-consciousness inference does not require language when neurobiology and species-appropriate behavior converge.**
25. **Strong consciousness evidence can arise in an independently evolved nervous-system architecture, as in cephalopods.**
26. **Behavioral unresponsiveness is not equivalent to absence of consciousness.**
27. **Reduced responsiveness or communication does not imply proportionally reduced consciousness probability when independent awareness evidence is present.**
28. **The strongest external consciousness inference comes from convergence across report, behavior, neurobiology, causal intervention, state transitions, and theory-relevant mechanisms—not one marker.**
