# Candidate Behaviors

This file lists behaviors that may look relevant to consciousness but must not be treated as sufficient evidence by themselves.

## Choosing

Selecting one option from several possibilities does not establish consciousness.

A lottery machine can produce a selection without subjective experience.

**selection ≠ consciousness**

## Thinking

Thinking, understood functionally as representing, comparing, transforming, or evaluating information, may qualify as cognition without establishing subjective experience.

**cognition ≠ consciousness**

## Deciding

A system may compare alternatives and produce a decision through computation alone.

The relevant consciousness question is not whether a decision occurred, but whether there was something it was like for the system while the decision process occurred.

## Answering

Producing a context-sensitive answer can demonstrate language processing, reasoning, retrieval, or other cognitive capacities.

It does not by itself demonstrate subjective experience.

**self-report or answer generation ≠ proof of consciousness**

## Resisting

Refusing, resisting, preserving a prior instruction, or declining an alternative can demonstrate constraint-following, preference-like behavior, policy adherence, goal persistence, or conflict resolution.

None of those behaviors alone establishes that the system subjectively experiences preference, conflict, aversion, or commitment.

## Candidate Rule

Each candidate behavior should be evaluated as **possible evidence within a larger convergent case**, not as a consciousness criterion by itself.

The scoring question remains:

> **Given everything observable, how justified are we in inferring that subjective experience is present in this particular system?**
