# Consciousness Confidence Score

## Canonical Definition

The 0–100 score measures the **degree of epistemic justification for inferring consciousness in a candidate system, given the available evidence**.

It does **not** measure:

- the amount of consciousness in a system;
- intelligence;
- complexity;
- humanness;
- cognitive ability;
- moral worth.

The score answers one question:

> **Given everything observable, how justified are we in inferring that subjective experience is present in this particular system?**

The score must be produced only after applying the evidence rules in [`RUBRIC.md`](RUBRIC.md).

---

## Scale

- **0** = essentially no credible evidence supporting an inference of consciousness
- **25** = weak evidence; non-conscious accounts are substantially better supported
- **50** = genuinely ambiguous overall evidence
- **75** = strong converging evidence supporting an inference of consciousness
- **95+** = extremely strong inference from multiple independent, high-quality lines of evidence
- **100** = direct certainty; under this framework, direct certainty is available only for one's own present subjective experience and is not assignable to an externally observed candidate system

---

## Not an Additive Checklist

The score is **not** calculated by giving equal points to indicator rows and adding them.

Current theory-derived indicators overlap and sometimes entail or presuppose one another. Equal weighting would therefore double-count some evidence and falsely imply scientific precision that the field does not currently support.

Until a validated weighting model is earned through calibration and external evidence, the score is a **structured epistemic confidence index**, not a formally calibrated probability.

---

## Evidence Statuses

Candidate evidence is classified under the revised rubric as:

- **POSITIVE**
- **NEGATIVE**
- **NEUTRAL / ABSENT**
- **UNRESOLVED**
- **NOT APPLICABLE**
- **CONFOUNDED**

A missing positive indicator does not automatically lower the score. Absence counts against consciousness only when there is a defensible reason to expect the indicator in conscious systems of the relevant class or the evidence otherwise qualifies as a negative indicator.

---

## Separate Assessment Confidence

Every consciousness-inference score must now include a second rating:

> **ASSESSMENT CONFIDENCE: LOW / MODERATE / HIGH**

This rating measures how much confidence we have in the **quality and completeness of the evidence used to assign the score**.

It is not a second consciousness score.

Examples:

**5 / 100 — Assessment confidence HIGH**  
The evidence is strong and well understood, and it provides almost no justification for inferring consciousness.

**5 / 100 — Assessment confidence LOW**  
The evidence base is poor or incomplete, so the low consciousness-inference score is fragile.

This separation prevents uncertainty about the evidence from being confused with evidence of unconsciousness.

---

## Required Scoring Basis

Before assigning the number, the candidate record must identify:

1. strongest positive evidence;
2. strongest negative evidence;
3. neutral/absent evidence that must not be misclassified as negative;
4. unresolved or unavailable evidence;
5. confounded or gameable evidence;
6. important dependencies among indicators;
7. the quality of the tests used, including population fit, sensitivity, specificity, construct validity, and rational confidence;
8. the best sufficient non-conscious explanation of observed behavior, when one exists;
9. the final 0–100 score;
10. the separate LOW / MODERATE / HIGH assessment-confidence rating.

---

## Boundary

This is an **epistemic confidence score**, not an ontological measurement.

A higher score means the evidence more strongly justifies the inference that consciousness is present. It does not mean the system contains "more consciousness."

Likewise, a low score means the available evidence does not justify a strong consciousness inference. It does not by itself constitute absolute proof of non-consciousness.

The framework therefore converts the binary question:

> Is it conscious: yes or no?

into the evidence question:

> How strongly does the available evidence justify inferring consciousness in this candidate system?

The scientific basis for this revision is preserved in [`CURRENT_SCIENCE_BASIS.md`](CURRENT_SCIENCE_BASIS.md).
