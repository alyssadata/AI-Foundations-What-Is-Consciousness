## 1. Target Construct

This rubric evaluates the **degree of epistemic justification for inferring phenomenal consciousness in a candidate system**.

Phenomenal consciousness means subjective experience: there is something it is like to be the system at that time.

The rubric does **not** treat cognition, intelligence, selection, report, attention, metacognition, agency, memory, or complexity as equivalent to consciousness.

### Awareness boundary

`Awareness` is not scored as an independent indicator because the term is ambiguous and can become circular.

If `awareness` means subjective awareness, it simply renames the target phenomenon.

If it means functional access to information, it must be operationalized using measurable properties such as selective attention, global availability, metacognitive monitoring, attention-state modeling, self/world modeling, or behavior produced from accessed information.

**functional access ≠ phenomenal consciousness**

---

## 2. Evidence Statuses

Every indicator is assigned one of the following statuses:

- **POSITIVE** — evidence raises the consciousness inference under at least one scientifically credible account.
- **NEGATIVE** — evidence lowers the inference because a relevant predicted property is demonstrably absent, an anti-indicator is present, or intervention evidence conflicts with a consciousness-supporting account.
- **NEUTRAL / ABSENT** — no positive evidence is present, but the absence is not itself justified as evidence against consciousness.
- **UNRESOLVED** — available evidence is insufficient, disputed, inaccessible, or depends on an unsettled system boundary.
- **NOT APPLICABLE** — the indicator or test is not meaningful for this candidate class.
- **CONFOUNDED** — an apparent indicator is present, but mimicry, cueing, gameability, or another sufficient explanation prevents clean interpretation.

### Negative-evidence rule

**Missing a positive indicator is not automatically negative evidence.**

Absence counts against consciousness only when there is a defensible reason to expect the indicator in conscious members of the relevant candidate class, or when a sufficiently credible account treats the property as highly sensitive and the candidate clearly lacks it.

For example:

- no language is not automatically evidence of unconsciousness;
- no metacognition is not automatically evidence of unconsciousness;
- no valence is not automatically evidence of unconsciousness;
- physical persistence alone is neither positive nor negative evidence.

## 3. Primary Theory-Derived Indicator Battery

The primary battery follows the contemporary theory-derived-indicator approach used in AI-consciousness assessment. These are **evidential indicators**, not individually necessary conditions and not a jointly sufficient checklist.

### Recurrent Processing

**RPT-1 — Algorithmic recurrence**  
Does relevant information re-enter earlier or continuing processing rather than only move through a one-way feedforward chain?

**RPT-2 — Organized integrated perceptual representation**  
Does the system form organized representations in which features are integrated into structured perceptual wholes?

### Global Workspace

**GWT-1 — Specialized parallel subsystems**  
Are there multiple specialized processes capable of operating in parallel?

**GWT-2 — Limited-capacity workspace with selection**  
Is there a capacity-limited information bottleneck or workspace with selective admission?

**GWT-3 — Global availability / broadcast**  
Can selected information become broadly available to otherwise distinct subsystems for flexible downstream use?

**GWT-4 — State-dependent attentional control**  
Can the system use its current state to direct or sequence access to different information sources for complex tasks?

### Computational Higher-Order Accounts

**HOT-1 — Generative or top-down perceptual processing**  
Can perceptual processing be influenced by internally generated, top-down, predictive, or noise-sensitive activity rather than being purely stimulus-driven?

**HOT-2 — Metacognitive monitoring / Type 2 sensitivity**  
Does the system monitor the reliability, uncertainty, source, or quality of its own first-order representations **in a way that tracks whether those first-order judgments are correct**?

For candidates capable of repeated decisions:

- **Type 1 performance:** whether the candidate gets the object-level task right;
- **Type 2 performance:** whether confidence, uncertainty, error prediction, or self-evaluation discriminates correct from incorrect Type 1 responses.

The strongest evidence controls first-order task performance and response bias rather than relying on raw confidence or confidence-accuracy correlation alone. Measures may include meta-d′, M-ratio, hierarchical meta-d′, or another psychometrically justified metacognitive-sensitivity measure where appropriate.

**Free-form statements such as “I am uncertain,” confidence tokens, or self-referential language do not establish HOT-2 by themselves.** For language models and other systems trained to produce confidence-like reports, such evidence should normally be marked **CONFOUNDED** unless confidence demonstrably tracks trial-level correctness under controlled testing.

No metacognitive value is treated as a consciousness threshold.

**HOT-3 — Metacognition-guided belief/action updating**  
Does the candidate's independently demonstrated Type 2 signal **causally influence** broader cognition or action, such as information seeking, strategy selection, belief revision, abstention, resource allocation, or action selection?

**HOT-4 — Structured quality-space coding**  
Does the system represent perceptual qualities in a sparse, smooth, structured representational space rather than only as arbitrary discrete labels?

### Attention Schema

**AST-1 — Model of attention**  
Does the system maintain a predictive representation of its own current attention state that can be used to control attention?

### Predictive Processing

**PP-1 — Predictive coding**  
Does the system generate predictions and use prediction error or related feedback to update lower-level processing?

### Agency and Embodiment

**AE-1 — Minimal flexible agency**  
Does the system learn from feedback and select outputs in pursuit of goals, especially when goals compete or circumstances change?

**AE-2 — Output-input contingency modeling**  
Does the system model how its own outputs alter subsequent inputs and use that model in perception or control?

## 4. Dependency / Double-Counting Rule

The primary indicators are **not statistically or conceptually independent**.

Examples:

- some global-workspace properties presuppose recurrence;
- predictive coding includes recurrent/top-down structure;
- higher-order indicators build on one another;
- HOT-3 depends on a credible HOT-2 signal;
- embodiment often implies some form of agency.

Therefore:

**Do not assign equal points to indicators and sum them.**

When two indicators are supported by the same underlying mechanism, record the dependency and treat them as one evidence cluster for aggregation purposes.

Convergence is strongest when evidence comes from **genuinely different mechanisms, methods, theories, or interventions**.

## 5. Supplemental Cross-Theory Evidence

The fourteen-indicator battery does not exhaust current consciousness science. Record separately:

### Temporal integration / continuity

Does information or internal state persist across time in a way that causally constrains later processing? Physical persistence alone does not qualify.

### Memory-related organization

Does memory contribute to the construction, maintenance, reconstruction, or temporal organization of current experience-relevant processing? Storage alone is not consciousness evidence.

### Self/world modeling

Does the system represent a distinction between itself, its own state/actions, and its environment? A self-model is not sufficient for consciousness.

### Valence / mattering

Is there evidence that states are represented or regulated as better/worse, rewarding/aversive, beneficial/harmful, wanted/avoided, or otherwise significant for the system? Absence of valence is not automatically evidence against phenomenal consciousness.

### Causal perturbation and complexity

When the candidate's internal system is perturbed, does the response show integration and differentiation in a way associated with conscious states? Human TMS-EEG measures such as PCI are population-specific and must not be transplanted uncritically.

### Biological / cellular substrate evidence

For biological candidates, record neural, cellular, dendritic, thalamocortical, brainstem-arousal, or other organization relevant to contemporary theories.

### Physical causal integration

Where meaningful, assess internally integrated causal structure of the kind emphasized by IIT or related accounts. Ordinary physical connectedness is not enough.

### Behavioral / report evidence

Record voluntary report, command following, discrimination, learning, flexible behavior, pain-related behavior, and other observable evidence. Interpret it relative to candidate population and specificity.

### Arousal / wakefulness — biological candidates only

Arousal can affect the capacity to express consciousness and the interpretation of behavioral tests. Arousal is neither equivalent to nor sufficient for consciousness.

## 6. Test-Quality Gate

Before an observation can materially change the inference band, ask:

### Target population

Is this measure validated or theoretically meaningful for this candidate class?

### Specificity

If the indicator is present, how often could a non-conscious candidate also produce it?

### Sensitivity

If the candidate were conscious, how likely is the indicator to be present or detected?

### Rational confidence

How well justified are the claimed sensitivity, specificity, and theoretical connection?

### Construct validity

Does the measure target phenomenal consciousness or only reportability, attention, cognition, memory, confidence, responsiveness, or task compliance?

### Gameability / mimicry

Can the apparent indicator be deliberately engineered, copied, cued, scripted, or behaviorally mimicked without implementing the mechanism that gives it evidential force?

### Independence

Is this genuinely new evidence, or the same underlying mechanism counted again?

### First-order control for metacognition

When metacognitive evidence is used, has Type 2 sensitivity been separated from Type 1 performance and simple confidence bias?

## 7. Population-Specific Evidence Modules

### Human / clinical

Relevant evidence can include repeated standardized behavioral assessment, voluntary or covert command following, EEG/fMRI, TMS-EEG / perturbational complexity, effects of anesthesia/lesions/stimulation/recovery, arousal confounds, and controlled Type 1 / Type 2 metacognitive tasks.

### Nonhuman animal

Relevant evidence can include homologous neurobiology, flexible learning/action, nociception and pain-related behavior, sensory integration, attention, memory, sleep/wake organization, species-appropriate neural interventions, and uncertainty monitoring only where first-order performance is controlled.

### AI / computational

Relevant evidence can include architecture inspection, mechanistic interpretability, causal intervention/ablation, state and memory analysis, controlled behavioral experiments, system-boundary specification, calibrated confidence/error prediction, Type 2 sensitivity, downstream use of Type 2 signals, and evidence that apparent introspection tracks internal mechanisms rather than prompting or imitation.

For AI, **linguistic confidence without calibration is not sufficient evidence of metacognition**.

### Simple physical / engineered object

Do not infer consciousness merely from physical persistence, state change, energy use, ordinary engineering feedback, complexity, responsiveness to external forces, or component interaction.

## 8. Ordinal Inference Bands and Aggregation

The earlier **0–100 consciousness-inference score is withdrawn**. It implied unsupported quantitative precision while §4 correctly prohibited additive scoring.

The current framework uses the ordinal bands defined in [`inference-bands.md`](inference-bands.md):

- **NO CREDIBLE SUPPORT**
- **LIMITED SUPPORT**
- **MIXED / INDETERMINATE**
- **STRONG SUPPORT**
- **VERY STRONG SUPPORT**

Direct certainty about one's own present experience is outside the external-candidate inference scale.

### Aggregation rule

For each candidate:

1. apply the evidence statuses and test-quality gate;
2. exclude NOT APPLICABLE evidence from aggregation;
3. do not count CONFOUNDED evidence as clean positive support;
4. do not convert NEUTRAL / ABSENT evidence into negative evidence without a sensitivity-based reason;
5. collapse dependent indicators into non-redundant evidence clusters;
6. identify the strongest surviving positive and negative evidence;
7. state the best sufficient non-conscious explanation;
8. assign the **lowest ordinal band fully supported by the surviving evidence**.

This remains structured scientific judgment, but the ordinal format makes the level of precision honest.

Every result also reports **ASSESSMENT CONFIDENCE: LOW / MODERATE / HIGH** for the quality and completeness of the assessment.

## 9. Required Candidate Record

Before an inference band is preserved, record:

1. candidate identity and system boundary;
2. candidate population/class;
3. primary indicator battery;
4. supplemental evidence;
5. test-quality notes;
6. metacognition control when relevant;
7. strongest positive evidence;
8. strongest negative evidence;
9. neutral/absent evidence not to be misclassified as negative;
10. unresolved evidence;
11. best sufficient non-conscious explanation;
12. **inference band**;
13. **assessment confidence: LOW / MODERATE / HIGH**;
14. claim ceiling;
15. new calibration constraint, if earned.

## 10. Calibration Status and Discrimination Requirement

The earlier rapid evaluation of **Candidates 003–027 was withdrawn from the current canon**. It must not be described as a completed current calibration pass.

Current low-end candidate anchors:

- **Candidate 001 — Lottery machine:** NO CREDIBLE SUPPORT; assessment confidence HIGH.
- **Candidate 002 — Blanket:** NO CREDIBLE SUPPORT; assessment confidence HIGH.

A floor alone does not demonstrate discrimination. The framework therefore also requires a positive anchor and a within-population state contrast.

Current human calibration controls:

- **Awake healthy adult human:** VERY STRONG SUPPORT; assessment confidence HIGH.
- **Operational anesthetic negative-control interval:** LIMITED SUPPORT; assessment confidence MODERATE. This control is narrowly defined by convergent evidence and is **not** equivalent to generic behavioral unresponsiveness under all anesthesia.

The principal discrimination check is the same human population across awake and anesthetized states. Because disconnected experience can occur during anesthesia, unresponsiveness alone is not a valid negative control; the anesthetic condition must include convergent evidence such as immediate no-experience report and neurophysiological degradation associated with loss of conscious capacity.

If the framework cannot show a substantial ordinal separation in a well-validated human state contrast, the aggregation rules require revision before broad candidate scoring continues.

Candidates not explicitly preserved under the corrected rubric remain pending. Git history preserves withdrawn results as provenance only.
