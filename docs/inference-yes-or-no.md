# Evidence That Shifts the Consciousness Inference

The evidence shifts the strength of the inference; it does not directly reveal subjective experience.

This file defines **evidential direction**. The full operational framework is in [`RUBRIC.md`](RUBRIC.md).

---

## Evidence Statuses

Evidence must be classified as one of:

- **POSITIVE** — raises the consciousness inference;
- **NEGATIVE** — lowers the consciousness inference;
- **NEUTRAL / ABSENT** — provides no positive evidence but is not justified as negative evidence;
- **UNRESOLVED** — the relevant fact or its interpretation is unknown or disputed;
- **NOT APPLICABLE** — the measure is not meaningful for this candidate class;
- **CONFOUNDED** — an apparent indicator is present but cannot be cleanly interpreted because of mimicry, cueing, gameability, or another sufficient explanation.

---

## Evidence That Can Push Toward Consciousness

Depending on candidate class and evidence quality, positive evidence can include:

- recurrent processing that contributes to integrated representations;
- organized and integrated perceptual representations;
- selective information access through a limited-capacity workspace;
- broad/global availability of selected information;
- state-dependent attentional control;
- metacognitive monitoring with demonstrated Type 2 sensitivity;
- metacognition that causally changes belief, decision, or action;
- predictive attention-state modeling;
- predictive coding and error-driven updating;
- flexible agency that learns from feedback and handles competing goals;
- modeling of output-input / sensorimotor contingencies;
- temporally integrated state that causally constrains later processing;
- self/world modeling;
- credible valence or mattering evidence;
- biological or causal organization associated with conscious states;
- perturbational or intervention evidence showing consciousness-relevant integration and differentiation;
- voluntary report or command following when the behavior has high specificity for consciousness in the relevant population;
- reliable degradation or restoration of consciousness-relevant capacities when candidate mechanisms are disrupted or restored.

No item in this list is automatically decisive.

---

## Evidence That Can Push Away From Consciousness

Negative evidence is narrower than the first-pass rubric implied.

Examples include:

- demonstrable absence of a property that a sufficiently credible theory treats as necessary or highly sensitive for consciousness in that candidate class;
- a negative indicator independently predicted to lower consciousness credence;
- causal intervention showing that an alleged consciousness-supporting mechanism is inert, epiphenomenal, or unnecessary for the relevant capacity;
- apparent consciousness-like behavior that disappears when superficial cue-following, pattern matching, scripting, or imitation is controlled for;
- strong evidence that a claimed internal indicator is not actually implemented;
- strong state-dependent degradation of validated consciousness-relevant measures in a population where those measures have appropriate construct validity.

---

## What Does NOT Automatically Count Against Consciousness

The following absences must normally be recorded as **NEUTRAL / ABSENT**, not automatically as negative:

- no language;
- no self-report;
- no sophisticated metacognition;
- no human-like self-concept;
- no demonstrated valence;
- no human-like intelligence;
- no biological brain, unless a biological-substrate theory is specifically being evaluated;
- no physical persistence beyond what the candidate type requires.

A conscious system could in principle lack several of these properties.

---

## Awareness Boundary

`Awareness` is not treated as a free-standing evidence category.

If awareness means phenomenal awareness, it is the target phenomenon and treating it as an indicator would be circular.

If awareness means functional access, attention, metacognition, self-monitoring, or availability for action, it must be recorded under the corresponding operational indicator.

---

## Mechanistic Explanation Boundary

A mechanistic explanation is not automatically evidence against consciousness. Conscious biological systems also have mechanisms.

A simple non-conscious explanation matters when it fully accounts for the observed consciousness-like behavior **and** there is no independent consciousness-supporting evidence that requires a richer interpretation.

Therefore:

**sufficient non-conscious explanation → weakens the evidential value of the behavior**

but not:

**mechanistic explanation → unconscious**

---

## Sensitivity / Specificity Rule

The evidential meaning of presence and absence depends on the test.

- A highly **specific** positive indicator can strongly support consciousness even if many conscious systems lack it.
- A highly **sensitive** indicator can make its clear absence informative even if many non-conscious systems also possess it.
- A test with unknown specificity or sensitivity must not be treated as decisive.

The relevant sensitivity and specificity are **population-dependent**. A behavior that is highly specific for consciousness in a human patient may have poor specificity in an AI system.

---

## Convergence Rule

The strongest inference comes from multiple **non-redundant** lines of evidence.

**independent high-quality indicators pointing toward consciousness → stronger positive inference**  
**independent high-quality negative indicators → stronger negative inference**

Indicators that share the same underlying mechanism must not be counted as independent evidence merely because they have different names.
