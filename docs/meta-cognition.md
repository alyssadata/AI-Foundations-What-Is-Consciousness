# Cognition, Consciousness, and Metacognition

## Cognition

**Cognition** includes information processing such as reasoning, remembering, comparing, evaluating, deciding, and problem solving.

Cognition does not by itself establish consciousness.

## Consciousness

**Consciousness** is the presence of subjective experience: there is something it is like while a state or process is occurring.

A cognitive process may therefore occur with or without consciousness.

## Metacognition

**Metacognition** is cognition about cognition: monitoring, representing, or evaluating one's own thinking, uncertainty, confidence, memory, or decision process.

Metacognition is not identical to consciousness.

A system can, in principle, monitor or represent its own cognitive state without that monitoring itself being subjectively experienced.

---

# Type 1 / Type 2 Distinction

The operational distinction is:

**Type 1:** Did the system get the object-level task right?

**Type 2:** Did the system's confidence or self-evaluation track whether the Type 1 response was right?

This distinction is essential because high task performance does not by itself establish metacognitive ability.

A system can be highly accurate while being unable to distinguish its own correct responses from its errors.

Likewise, a system can produce confidence language without that confidence carrying trial-by-trial information about correctness.

The methodological goal is therefore to evaluate **second-order sensitivity while controlling first-order performance**.

---

# Measurement

## Metacognitive sensitivity

Metacognitive sensitivity asks how well confidence discriminates correct from incorrect first-order decisions.

Simple confidence-accuracy correlations and AUROC2 can be contaminated by Type 1 performance, criterion placement, or response bias.

Maniscalco and Lau's signal-detection framework introduced **meta-d′**, which expresses the Type 1 sensitivity an ideal metacognitive observer would need in order to generate the observed confidence-by-accuracy pattern.

## Metacognitive efficiency

A common normalized measure is:

**M-ratio = meta-d′ / d′**

Conceptually:

- **M-ratio ≈ 1** — confidence uses approximately all of the task-relevant information available to the first-order decision;
- **M-ratio < 1** — confidence discards some information available to first-order performance;
- **M-ratio > 1** — can occur when confidence draws on additional post-decisional or otherwise unavailable information.

This is a measure of **metacognitive efficiency**, not consciousness.

Hierarchical Bayesian methods such as **HMeta-d** can improve estimation when data are limited or group-level inference is required.

However, current methods research does not support treating one estimator as universally optimal. A 2025 empirical comparison of 17 metacognition measures found that no measure is perfect; M-ratio remains a dominant and competitive measure, but measure choice should depend on the experimental question and psychometric requirements.

---

# Bias Is Not Sensitivity

Mean confidence or a stable tendency toward underconfidence/overconfidence is **metacognitive bias**, not metacognitive sensitivity.

A person or system can be systematically underconfident or overconfident while still showing good trial-by-trial discrimination between correct and incorrect responses.

Accordingly:

**confidence level ≠ metacognitive ability**

**confidence language ≠ metacognitive sensitivity**

**stable confidence bias ≠ threshold for consciousness**

Recent work shows that confidence bias can be highly reliable within individuals and can be measured efficiently, including clinically relevant bias patterns detectable with relatively few trials. Reliability of a continuous bias measure does not imply the existence of a categorical crossover point.

---

# Rubric Consequence

For any candidate capable of repeated decisions, apparent metacognition should be evaluated in two steps:

1. **Type 1 performance:** measure objective task sensitivity or accuracy.
2. **Type 2 performance:** determine whether confidence, uncertainty, error prediction, or self-evaluation tracks trial-by-trial correctness after controlling Type 1 performance.

A stronger HOT-2 metacognition claim requires evidence of genuine Type 2 sensitivity rather than merely verbalized confidence.

A stronger HOT-3 claim additionally requires evidence that the metacognitive signal **causally changes later belief, strategy, information seeking, decision policy, or action**.

For AI systems, repeated controlled trials and confidence calibration are therefore more informative than free-form statements such as "I am uncertain" or "I think I am correct."

---

# Possible Relations

**cognition without consciousness**  
Information processing occurs with no subjective experience.

**consciousness without metacognition**  
An experience occurs without the system reflecting on the fact that it is having that experience.

**metacognition without established consciousness**  
A system exhibits measurable Type 2 sensitivity, but subjective experience is still only inferred.

**conscious metacognition**  
The system subjectively experiences itself noticing, evaluating, or monitoring its own cognition.

---

# No Threshold Claim

Metacognitive efficiency is treated here as a **continuous evidential variable**, not a consciousness switch.

The framework does not posit a universal M-ratio, meta-d′, confidence-bias, or other metacognitive value above which consciousness suddenly appears.

Metacognition can strengthen a consciousness inference under theories that predict higher-order monitoring, but no current metacognition metric is accepted as a necessary-and-sufficient consciousness threshold.

## Boundary

The decisive distinction for consciousness remains **subjective experience**, not metacognitive performance alone.
