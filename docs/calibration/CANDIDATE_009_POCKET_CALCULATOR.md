## 1. Candidate Identity and System Boundary

The candidate is an **ordinary basic non-programmable pocket calculator** operating normally.

The evaluated system includes:

- keypad and input-encoding circuitry;
- calculator ASIC or narrow fixed-function processor;
- arithmetic logic;
- registers or equivalent temporary internal state;
- fixed operation/control logic;
- display circuitry and numerical output;
- ordinary error/overflow states where present.

It excludes:

- programmable calculators;
- graphing calculators;
- computer algebra systems;
- network access;
- general-purpose operating systems;
- language models or other AI systems;
- the human user who supplies the problem and interprets the result.

This boundary is chosen because the calculator genuinely performs **rule-governed computation over internally used numerical states** while remaining a narrow fixed-function system.

---

## 2. Candidate Population / Class

**Class:** simple fixed-function computational system

Relevant rubric module: **AI / computational module**, applied cautiously because the calculator is computational but far simpler than the systems for which contemporary AI-consciousness indicators are usually discussed.

The calculator advances beyond Candidate 008 in a meaningful way. A scale measures an external quantity and displays an estimate. A calculator instead receives encoded symbols, maintains operands and intermediate values, applies arithmetic rules, and produces a result that depends on those internal computational states.

The question is whether **internal representation plus correct computation** supplies evidence of subjective experience.

### Primary-battery result

The calculator contains **genuine internally used computational states** and performs **genuine rule-governed symbolic/numerical computation**.

Those properties are stronger than observer-readable display alone.

However, **none currently survives the test-quality gate as clean positive evidence of phenomenal consciousness**. The architecture supplies computation, not evidence that the computation is experienced.

## 4. Supplemental Cross-Theory Evidence

### Temporal integration / continuity

**Status:** FUNCTIONALLY PRESENT; CONFOUNDED FOR CONSCIOUSNESS

Multi-step arithmetic requires earlier input and intermediate states to constrain later processing. A partial sum, carry state, stored operand, or pending operation can causally determine the next computation.

This is real computational continuity.

It does not establish experienced continuity.

### Memory-related organization

**Status:** FUNCTIONALLY PRESENT; CONFOUNDED FOR CONSCIOUSNESS

Registers and temporary state can retain operands, intermediate results, memory-register values, and operation context.

This qualifies as functional computational memory. It is not evidence of remembered experience or of memory constructing a subjective scene.

### Self/world modeling

**Status:** NEUTRAL / ABSENT

The calculator represents operands and operations for computation but does not represent itself as a system distinct from an external environment.

### Valence / mattering

**Status:** NEUTRAL / ABSENT

Correct results, overflow, low battery, invalid operations, or hardware failure can be good or bad relative to the calculator's designed function. No evidence shows these states are represented as good/bad, wanted/avoided, pleasant/painful, or otherwise significant **for the calculator**.

### Causal perturbation and complexity

**Status:** UNRESOLVED / LOW SPECIFICITY

The calculator's internal circuitry can be causally perturbed and its computational behavior changes predictably. No validated perturbational consciousness metric maps this narrow digital architecture to phenomenal experience.

### Biological / cellular substrate evidence

**Status:** NOT APPLICABLE

The calculator has no biological nervous or cellular substrate relevant to biological consciousness theories.

### Physical causal integration

**Status:** UNRESOLVED

The calculator has structured causal organization among input encoding, control logic, registers, arithmetic circuitry, and display. No candidate-specific substrate-neutral analysis establishes that this causal organization supports consciousness rather than ordinary computation.

### Behavioral / report evidence

**Status:** CONFOUNDED / NEUTRAL

The calculator can produce highly reliable correct answers to arithmetic problems within its designed domain.

Correctness is genuine. The device performs transformations that humans describe semantically as addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, percentages, and related operations.

But producing the correct result does not establish that the calculator understands the numbers, experiences the problem, or reports a subjective state.

### Arousal / wakefulness

**Status:** NOT APPLICABLE

## 5. Test-Quality Gate

### Target population

Computational consciousness indicators are not validated as a universal test for narrow fixed-function arithmetic devices. Apparent indicator matches must therefore preserve the theory's functional meaning rather than rely on vocabulary such as `processing`, `memory`, or `representation` alone.

### Specificity

Correct arithmetic, internal registers, stateful computation, error flags, symbolic encoding, and deterministic algorithm execution have **very low demonstrated specificity for phenomenal consciousness**.

They are intentionally realizable by narrow digital systems without any independent evidence of experience.

### Sensitivity

Unknown for arbitrary engineered substrates. This prevents a universal claim that a calculator's missing features prove unconsciousness, but it does not turn computation itself into positive evidence.

### Rational confidence

High confidence in the functional architecture of an ordinary basic calculator and high confidence that no consciousness-specific attentional, metacognitive, perceptual, agentic, or phenomenological mechanism has been identified in that architecture.

### Construct validity

The main construct errors would be treating:

- computation as consciousness;
- internal numerical coding as phenomenal representation;
- correct output as understanding;
- temporary registers as experienced memory;
- sequential processing as conscious recurrence;
- error flags as metacognition;
- a display as subjective report.

### Gameability / mimicry

The central confound is **formal competence without evidence of experience**. A fixed arithmetic circuit can produce exactly the result associated with a mathematical operation because its physical state transitions implement the required rule.

Behavioral correctness therefore cannot be granted consciousness-specific weight without additional evidence about the architecture and its internal organization.

### Independence

Input encoding, stored operands, arithmetic operations, intermediate states, error handling, and displayed results are tightly connected stages of one fixed computational mechanism and must not be counted as independent consciousness evidence.

## 6. Strongest Positive Evidence

The strongest potentially relevant evidence is the calculator's **internal use of representational computational state**:

- encoded operands;
- operation codes;
- stored intermediate values;
- rule-governed transformations;
- results that depend causally on those internal states.

This is stronger than an observer merely assigning meaning to a physical display. The internal states have functional roles within the computation.

However, under the current rubric they remain **insufficient as consciousness evidence** because no mechanism connects those functional representations to perception, global cognitive availability, metacognition, valence, agency, or subjective experience.

## 7. Strongest Negative Evidence

No decisive candidate-specific negative evidence is required.

The low score rests on the absence of credible consciousness-specific evidence after granting the calculator its genuine computational and representational capacities rather than explaining them away.

## 9. Unresolved Evidence

No universal substrate-neutral consciousness test has been validated for a basic pocket calculator.

Some theories of consciousness assign importance to computational or causal organization. The present evaluation does not establish that every possible realization of arithmetic computation is non-conscious.

The narrower conclusion is that the architecture and behavior of this candidate provide **no consciousness-specific evidence beyond computation itself**.

---

## 10. Best Sufficient Non-Conscious Explanation

The observed behavior is sufficiently explained by ordinary digital computation:

**key press → encoded operand/operator state → fixed control/arithmetic logic → register/intermediate-state updates → result register → numerical display**

The calculator can manipulate internally meaningful-for-function states and return correct arithmetic results without any additional requirement that those operations be experienced.

This explanation preserves the reality of the computation while showing that subjective experience is not needed to explain the observed competence.

## 13. Semantic Boundary

Candidate 009 also establishes:

> **Producing a correct result by formal transformation does not, by itself, establish semantic understanding of the symbols or quantities transformed.**

Compressed:

**correct manipulation ≠ experienced understanding**

This boundary does not deny that the calculator's internal states have functional representational roles. It separates **functional representation used in computation** from evidence that the represented content is understood or experienced by a subject.

---

## 14. Claim Ceiling

This calibration establishes Candidate 009 as a defensible **0 / 100 low-end epistemic anchor with HIGH assessment confidence** under the corrected rubric.

It supports the distinction between **computation performed by a system** and **phenomenal experience accompanying computation**.

It does **not** establish:

- that computation is irrelevant to consciousness;
- that computational systems cannot be conscious;
- that internal representation is never consciousness-relevant;
- that all calculators or computers must receive exactly 0;
- that semantic understanding can never be implemented computationally;
- or that a richer computational architecture with recurrence, global access, self-monitoring, learning, agency, and other converging evidence could not justify a higher inference.
