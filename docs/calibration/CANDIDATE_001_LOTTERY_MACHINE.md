## 1. Candidate Identity and System Boundary

The candidate is an ordinary mechanical lottery-ball machine whose function is to randomize and select numbered balls through airflow, agitation, gravity, gating, or comparable ordinary mechanisms.

Excluded: human operators, external cameras, viewers, and unrelated computers.

**Class:** simple engineered physical system.

## 4. Test Quality

The candidate mechanism is well understood. Random selection has extremely poor specificity for consciousness. The sensitivity of theory-derived indicators to every metaphysically possible conscious system is unknown, so absent indicators are not converted into universal proof of non-consciousness.

Physical agitation, randomness, outcome selection, and component interaction arise from the same mechanism and are not counted repeatedly.

**Strongest positive evidence:** none identified.

**Strongest negative evidence:** no separate negative indicator is required. This is a lack-of-positive-evidence anchor, not a pile of absent cognitive features counted as negatives.

**ASSESSMENT CONFIDENCE: HIGH**

Interpretation:

> The available evidence provides no credible positive support for inferring subjective experience in an ordinary mechanical lottery machine, and the low-end assessment is well constrained.

This is not a metaphysical proof that consciousness is impossible in the machine.

## 7. Preserved Calibration Constraints

> **Mere selection contributes no positive evidence of consciousness.**

More precisely:

> **Producing one outcome from multiple possibilities does not, by itself, raise the consciousness inference.**

And:

> **Ordinary physical persistence, component interaction, randomness, and state change must not be relabeled as consciousness-relevant information-processing indicators without evidence that the candidate implements the relevant mechanism.**
