# Revised-Rubric Revalidation

## 1. Candidate Identity and System Boundary

The candidate is an **ordinary mechanical lottery-ball machine** whose function is to randomize and select numbered balls through physical agitation, airflow, gravity, mechanical gating, or equivalent ordinary mechanisms.

The evaluated system includes the machine's ordinary physical components and balls during operation.

It excludes:

- human operators;
- external cameras or broadcast systems;
- external computers not required for the mechanical selection process;
- viewers interpreting the result.

**Candidate class:** simple engineered physical system.

## 2. Calibration Question

> **Given the full theory-derived indicator battery, supplemental evidence, test-quality constraints, and dependency corrections, how justified are we in inferring subjective experience in an ordinary mechanical lottery machine?**

## 5. Test-Quality Gate

### Target population

The candidate belongs to the **simple physical / engineered object** class. Brain-specific and animal-specific tests are not applied.

### Specificity

The salient observable behavior—random or pseudo-random physical selection—has **extremely poor specificity for consciousness** because ordinary non-conscious mechanisms routinely produce it.

### Sensitivity

The sensitivity of the fourteen theory-derived indicators to every metaphysically possible form of consciousness is unknown. Therefore, their absence is not converted into universal proof of non-consciousness.

### Rational confidence

Rational confidence is **high** that the machine's ordinary architecture and operation are correctly characterized and that mere random selection is not a consciousness-specific behavior.

### Construct validity

The observed selection behavior measures **outcome production**, not phenomenal consciousness.

### Gameability / mimicry

No sophisticated mimicry is required: the behavior is natively generated by a simple mechanism. This makes the behavior especially weak as consciousness evidence.

### Independence

Physical agitation, randomness, selection, and component interaction arise from the same ordinary mechanism and are not counted as independent consciousness indicators.

## 6. Strongest Positive Evidence

**None identified.**

No clean theory-derived, biological, behavioral, causal, valenced, metacognitive, representational, or agency-based evidence raises the consciousness inference.

## 7. Strongest Negative Evidence

No separate negative indicator is required to produce this calibration result.

The revised framework does **not** reason:

> no metacognition + no valence + no learning = unconscious.

Instead, Candidate 001 functions as a **lack-of-positive-evidence anchor**: the system is well characterized, but no credible evidence supports an inference of subjective experience.

---

## 8. Neutral / Absent Evidence That Must Not Be Misread as Negative

The following absences are recorded without treating them as individually decisive:

- metacognition;
- valence;
- learning;
- self/world modeling;
- predictive processing;
- global broadcast;
- temporal experience-relevant persistence.

Their importance is that they provide **no positive support**, not that each absence independently proves non-consciousness.

---

## 9. Unresolved Evidence

The framework does not claim to have measured every possible physical theory of consciousness in the machine.

In particular, no formal candidate-specific IIT quantity or universal physical consciousness measure has been established here.

That unresolved possibility does not itself supply positive evidence.

---

## 10. Best Sufficient Non-Conscious Explanation

The observed behavior is sufficiently explained by ordinary mechanics:

**physical agitation / airflow / gravity / gating → one numbered ball selected**

This explanation is relevant because it removes the evidential force of the selection behavior. It is **not** treated as proof that mechanistic systems cannot be conscious.

---

## 11. Revalidated Score

**CONSCIOUSNESS-INFERENCE SCORE: 0 / 100**  
**ASSESSMENT CONFIDENCE: HIGH**

Interpretation:

> **Given the available evidence, there is essentially no epistemic justification for inferring subjective experience in the ordinary mechanical lottery machine, and confidence in the completeness and quality of this low-end assessment is high.**

The score is not an ontological measurement and does not claim metaphysical proof that consciousness is impossible in the candidate.

---

## 12. Preserved Calibration Constraint

The original calibration constraint **survives revalidation**:

> **Mere selection contributes 0 positive evidence of consciousness.**

More precisely:

> **Producing one outcome from multiple possibilities does not, by itself, raise the consciousness inference.**

**selection ≠ consciousness**

---

## 13. Additional Calibration Constraint

The revised rubric also earns a second constraint:

> **Ordinary physical persistence, component interaction, randomness, and state change must not be relabeled as consciousness-relevant information-processing indicators without evidence that the candidate actually implements the relevant mechanism.**

This prevents verbal resemblance from becoming evidential double-counting.

---

## 14. Claim Ceiling

This calibration establishes Candidate 001 as a defensible **0 / 100 low-end epistemic anchor** under the revised rubric.

It supports the preserved rule that mere selection is not positive consciousness evidence.

It does **not** establish:

- a universal proof that lottery machines are metaphysically incapable of consciousness;
- that every system lacking one or more theory-derived indicators must score 0;
- that mechanistic explanation is incompatible with consciousness;
- or that the current theory-derived battery is a complete or validated universal detector.
