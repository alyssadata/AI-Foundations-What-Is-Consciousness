## 1. Candidate Identity and System Boundary

The candidate is an **ordinary incandescent lightbulb** connected to an ordinary electrical power source and operating normally.

The evaluated system includes:

- the glass envelope;
- the tungsten filament;
- the electrical contacts and support structure;
- the ordinary electrical and thermal dynamics occurring within the bulb while powered.

It excludes:

- smart bulbs;
- LEDs or fluorescent lamps with driver/control electronics;
- external dimmers, sensors, processors, timers, or network controllers;
- the electrical grid as a larger candidate system;
- humans or other external agents controlling the switch.

The incandescent case is used because its ordinary operating mechanism is comparatively simple and does not import hidden sensing, computation, or control architecture into the candidate boundary.

## 4. Supplemental Cross-Theory Evidence

### Temporal integration / continuity

**Status:** NEUTRAL

The filament has thermal inertia: its present temperature depends on immediately preceding electrical and thermal conditions. This is genuine physical state dependence, but no evidence connects it to experience-relevant temporal integration.

### Memory-related organization

**Status:** NEUTRAL / ABSENT

The bulb may retain heat briefly after power changes, and long-term use can alter the filament physically. These are physical traces and state persistence, not evidence of memory used to construct, reconstruct, or guide experience-relevant processing.

### Self/world modeling

**Status:** NEUTRAL / ABSENT

No evidence that the bulb represents itself, its internal state, its emitted light, or an external environment.

### Valence / mattering

**Status:** NEUTRAL / ABSENT

Operating temperature, power level, filament damage, and eventual burnout can be described as favorable or unfavorable from an engineering perspective, but there is no evidence that such states are represented as better/worse, wanted/avoided, pleasant/painful, or otherwise significant **for the bulb**.

### Causal perturbation and complexity

**Status:** NOT APPLICABLE / UNRESOLVED

The bulb can be perturbed by changing voltage, ambient temperature, vibration, or other physical conditions, and its response is measurable. Brain-derived consciousness measures such as PCI are not validated for incandescent bulbs and cannot be transferred to this candidate class.

### Biological / cellular substrate evidence

**Status:** NOT APPLICABLE

The bulb has no biological nervous, cellular, thalamocortical, brainstem, or comparable biological substrate.

### Physical causal integration

**Status:** UNRESOLVED

The bulb has ordinary causal coupling among voltage, current, resistance, filament temperature, radiation, and heat loss. No candidate-specific IIT or comparable substrate-neutral causal analysis has been established here.

Ordinary causal coupling is not treated as positive evidence merely because a system's variables interact.

### Behavioral / report evidence

**Status:** NEUTRAL / ABSENT

The bulb emits light and heat when electrical current raises the filament temperature. Brightness and spectrum vary systematically with operating conditions. These are reliable physical outputs, but there is no report, learned discrimination, flexible behavior, voluntary action, or other behavior with established specificity for consciousness in this candidate class.

### Arousal / wakefulness

**Status:** NOT APPLICABLE

## 5. Test-Quality Gate

### Target population

The main theory-derived indicators arise from neuroscientific and computational accounts and are not validated direct tests for incandescent bulbs. Missing indicators are therefore interpreted conservatively rather than automatically counted as negative evidence.

### Specificity

The salient behaviors of the candidate — receiving electrical energy, heating, changing resistance, emitting light, cooling, and eventually failing — have **extremely low specificity for consciousness**. They are fully ordinary properties of engineered physical systems.

### Sensitivity

Unknown for many theory-derived indicators when applied to arbitrary simple physical systems. This limits strong negative claims from absence alone.

### Rational confidence

High confidence in the ordinary operating mechanism of an incandescent bulb and in the absence of any identified consciousness-relevant representation, cognition, or agency within the defined system boundary. Lower confidence applies to universal metaphysical claims about all possible matter.

### Construct validity

Brightness, filament temperature, resistance, current, emitted spectrum, and thermal lag are not validated measures of phenomenal consciousness.

### Gameability / mimicry

The primary risk is semantic overinterpretation rather than deliberate mimicry: describing automatic input-output transformation as `sensing`, thermal lag as `memory`, temperature-dependent resistance as `feedback`, or coupled physics as `information integration` without demonstrating the consciousness-relevant mechanism.

### Independence

Electrical current, resistive heating, temperature-dependent resistance, light emission, and cooling are tightly coupled parts of the same operating mechanism and must not be counted as separate independent consciousness indicators.

## 10. Best Sufficient Non-Conscious Explanation

The observed operation of the candidate is sufficiently explained by ordinary electrodynamics and thermodynamics:

**applied voltage → electrical current through tungsten filament → resistive heating → high filament temperature → thermal radiation / visible light + heat loss**

Temperature-dependent resistance and thermal inertia account for additional state dependence without requiring representation, prediction, goal pursuit, or subjective experience.

This mechanistic account removes the evidential force of the bulb's reliable input-output behavior as a consciousness indicator. It does not claim that mechanistic explanation and consciousness are logically incompatible in general.

## 12. Preserved Calibration Constraint

Candidate 003 earns the following calibration constraint:

> **A reliable input → internal state → output transformation does not, by itself, provide positive evidence of consciousness.**

For this candidate:

**electrical input → filament heating → light output**

is a real causal transformation, but the existence of that transformation alone does not establish sensing, representation, cognition, awareness, or experience.

**reliable input-output transformation ≠ subjective experience**

---

## 13. Additional Boundary

> **Automatic physical feedback is not automatically consciousness-relevant feedback.**

The filament's resistance changes with temperature and thereby alters the electrical state of the circuit. That closed causal dependence is real, but it does not by itself establish prediction, error correction, representation, learning, or goal-directed control.

The word `feedback` is therefore not sufficient evidence without specifying what is represented, what is controlled, and how the mechanism bears on a consciousness-supporting account.

## 14. Claim Ceiling

This calibration establishes Candidate 003 as a defensible **0 / 100 low-end epistemic anchor** under the corrected rubric.

It supports the distinction between ordinary energy transduction and consciousness-relevant information processing.

It does **not** establish:

- metaphysical proof that all simple engineered systems are non-conscious;
- that every light-emitting system must receive exactly 0;
- that every form of feedback is irrelevant to consciousness;
- that non-biological substrates cannot support consciousness;
- or that future validated substrate-neutral theories could never identify consciousness-relevant properties in engineered systems.
