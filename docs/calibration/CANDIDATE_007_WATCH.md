## 1. Candidate Identity and System Boundary

The candidate is an **ordinary quartz analog wristwatch** operating normally.

The evaluated system includes:

- battery and power path;
- quartz oscillator;
- frequency-divider circuitry;
- stepper motor;
- gear train;
- hands and dial;
- the internal state transitions required for ordinary timekeeping.

It excludes:

- smartwatches;
- network connectivity;
- GPS, microphones, cameras, biometric sensors, or general-purpose processors;
- alarms or complex calendar functions unless separately specified;
- the human wearer or observer who reads the display.

The quartz analog case is used because it genuinely tracks elapsed time and exposes a stable, interpretable state while remaining a narrow fixed-function engineered system.

---

## 2. Candidate Population / Class

**Class:** simple engineered information-tracking / timing system

Relevant rubric module: **Simple physical / engineered object module**.

The watch is more structurally information-bearing than Candidates 001–005: its internal state is deliberately organized so that its output can be interpreted as time by an external user.

The question is whether that observer-relative representational role supplies evidence of **subjective experience in the watch itself**.

## 4. Supplemental Cross-Theory Evidence

### Temporal integration / continuity

**Status:** FUNCTIONALLY PRESENT; CONFOUNDED FOR CONSCIOUSNESS

The watch maintains temporal succession: earlier oscillator/divider states causally constrain later states, and the displayed time depends on accumulated state progression.

This is genuine functional temporal organization.

It does not by itself establish **experienced temporality**.

### Memory-related organization

**Status:** LIMITED FUNCTIONAL STATE; CONFOUNDED FOR CONSCIOUSNESS

The current gear/counter state preserves enough prior progression to determine the next displayed state. In a broad engineering sense, the system carries state across time.

That state is not evidence of episodic, working, autobiographical, or experience-constructing memory.

### Self/world modeling

**Status:** NEUTRAL / ABSENT

No evidence that the watch models itself, distinguishes self from world, or represents its own relation to an environment.

### Valence / mattering

**Status:** NEUTRAL / ABSENT

Accuracy, battery depletion, shock, water ingress, or component wear may be good or bad relative to the watch's designed function, but no evidence shows these states are represented as good/bad, wanted/avoided, pleasant/painful, or otherwise significant **for the watch**.

### Causal perturbation and complexity

**Status:** NOT APPLICABLE / UNRESOLVED

The watch can be perturbed physically and electrically, but no validated perturbational consciousness measure applies to a quartz wristwatch.

### Biological / cellular substrate evidence

**Status:** NOT APPLICABLE

The watch has no biological nervous or cellular substrate relevant to biological consciousness theories.

### Physical causal integration

**Status:** UNRESOLVED

The watch has organized causal relations among oscillator, divider, motor, gears, and hands. No candidate-specific substrate-neutral analysis establishes that this causal organization supports consciousness rather than ordinary engineered function.

### Behavioral / report evidence

**Status:** CONFOUNDED / NEUTRAL

The watch displays time accurately and responds predictably to setting inputs and power state.

Its display is **semantically interpretable to an observer**, but the watch does not report an experience. There is no evidence that the displayed symbols or hand positions are internally read, interpreted, or used as phenomenally available content by the system itself.

### Arousal / wakefulness

**Status:** NOT APPLICABLE

## 5. Test-Quality Gate

### Target population

Current consciousness indicators are not validated as direct tests for simple timekeeping devices. Missing indicators are therefore treated conservatively rather than converted into multiple negative strikes.

### Specificity

Accurate timing, periodic state transition, ordered internal state, and a readable display have **extremely low specificity for consciousness**. They can be implemented by simple deterministic mechanisms.

### Sensitivity

Unknown for arbitrary engineered substrates, but this uncertainty does not turn timekeeping itself into positive evidence.

### Rational confidence

High confidence in the mechanism and system boundary of an ordinary quartz watch, and high confidence that no consciousness-relevant representational, attentional, metacognitive, or agentic mechanism has been identified in that system.

### Construct validity

The main construct error would be treating:

- timekeeping as temporal awareness;
- a display as self-report;
- externally meaningful states as internally available representation;
- periodic recurrence as recurrent conscious processing;
- carried state as experienced memory.

### Gameability / mimicry

The central confound is **observer-relative semantics**. Humans designed the hand positions and dial markings to mean time. The fact that an external observer can interpret a state does not show the system itself interprets that state.

### Independence

Oscillation, division, motor stepping, gear movement, hand position, and displayed time are successive parts of one fixed timing mechanism and must not be double-counted as independent consciousness evidence.

## 6. Strongest Positive Evidence

The strongest potentially relevant property is the watch's **ordered internal state that carries temporal information and produces an externally interpretable representation of time**.

That is a real information-bearing function.

However, it remains **CONFOUNDED** as consciousness evidence because the semantic interpretation is supplied by the device's design and external users; no mechanism shows the watch itself accessing the state as represented content.

## 10. Best Sufficient Non-Conscious Explanation

The observed behavior is sufficiently explained by fixed timekeeping mechanics/electronics:

**quartz oscillation → frequency division → periodic motor steps → gear reduction → hand position → observer-readable time**

The system need not know what time it is in order to display a state that humans interpret as the time.

This explanation accounts for the candidate's apparent `representation` without requiring perception, semantic understanding, self-access, or subjective experience.

## 14. Claim Ceiling

This calibration establishes Candidate 007 as a defensible **0 / 100 low-end epistemic anchor with HIGH assessment confidence** under the corrected rubric.

It supports a distinction between **information carried by a system** and **information phenomenally or cognitively available to that system**.

It does **not** establish:

- that representation is irrelevant to consciousness;
- that engineered systems cannot be conscious;
- that every clock or watch architecture must receive exactly 0;
- that internal state is never consciousness-relevant;
- or that a future device with richer self-access, learning, modeling, or cognitive architecture could not supply different evidence.
