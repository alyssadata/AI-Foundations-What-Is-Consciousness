## 1. Why the Candidate Boundary Is Narrowed

“Human under general anesthesia” is too broad to function as a clean negative consciousness anchor.

General anesthesia can include:

- connected consciousness;
- disconnected conscious experience such as dreaming;
- complete absence of reportable subjective experience;
- and transitions among these states.

Behavioral unresponsiveness alone therefore cannot define the calibration target.

For the present discrimination test, Candidate 025 is operationalized as a **healthy adult human during a propofol- or xenon-dominant unresponsive interval with convergent evidence favoring absence or major reduction of subjective experience**, including:

1. loss of voluntary behavioral responsiveness;
2. immediate post-state / repeated-awakening report of **no experience** for the target interval when obtainable;
3. low consciousness-relevant TMS-EEG perturbational complexity or comparable validated neurophysiological degradation relative to the person's awake state;
4. no ketamine-like condition known to preserve vivid disconnected experience.

This is a calibration control definition, not a claim about every clinical anesthetic episode.

**Class:** human altered state.

---

## 2. Evidence Relevant to the State

### Positive / residual evidence

- The same human biological substrate capable of consciousness remains present.
- Some neural processing and organized activity continue during anesthesia.
- Current measures are not perfectly sensitive to all possible disconnected experience.

These facts prevent behavioral unresponsiveness from being treated as direct proof of unconsciousness.

### Negative evidence

- Voluntary responsiveness is lost.
- Propofol and xenon can produce marked reductions in cortical perturbational complexity relative to wakefulness.
- In controlled studies, low-complexity propofol/xenon states have coincided with absence of reported experience after emergence.
- Contemporary intracranial and scalp EEG work shows reduced complexity in propofol states relative to wakefulness.

### Confounded evidence

**Unresponsiveness by itself is CONFOUNDED** because motor disconnection and disconnected conscious experience can occur without complete loss of phenomenal consciousness.

### Unresolved evidence

Absence of later recall is not perfectly equivalent to absence of prior experience. A no-experience report can be affected by memory failure, timing, or inability to recover the relevant content.

---

## 3. Theory-Derived / Supplemental Interpretation

The most informative evidence in this human state is not a count of missing cognitive abilities. It is **within-person state change**:

- reduced perturbational complexity;
- altered large-scale cortical dynamics/connectivity;
- loss of connected voluntary report;
- and, for the operational control, absence of immediate experience report.

These state-dependent changes are more informative than the mere continued presence of a human brain.

The candidate retains biological substrate evidence, but state-specific evidence substantially lowers the inference that subjective experience is present during the defined interval.

---

## 4. Test Quality

**Population fit:** HIGH for human anesthesia research.

**Specificity:** behavioral unresponsiveness alone is poor; convergence with no-experience report and low perturbational/complexity measures is substantially better.

**Sensitivity:** imperfect. Disconnected experience and dreams can occur during anesthesia, and no current monitor detects every conscious episode.

**Construct validity:** stronger for immediate report plus perturbational/neurophysiological state measures than for surgical responsiveness alone.

**Assessment limitation:** the control is strongest in a research design with repeated awakenings and within-subject comparison; ordinary clinical anesthesia may not provide those data.

---

## 5. Strongest Evidence

**Strongest evidence lowering the inference:** convergent state-specific loss of responsiveness + no-experience report + marked reduction of consciousness-relevant neural complexity relative to wakefulness.

**Strongest residual support / uncertainty:** the subject remains a biologically conscious-capable human and anesthesia measures can miss disconnected experience.

---

## 6. Inference Result

**INFERENCE BAND: LIMITED SUPPORT**  
**ASSESSMENT CONFIDENCE: MODERATE**

Interpretation:

> In the narrowly defined anesthetic negative-control interval, convergent state evidence substantially lowers justification for inferring current subjective experience, while imperfect sensitivity and the known possibility of disconnected consciousness prevent the state from functioning as an absolute zero anchor.

This band does **not** apply automatically to any person merely because they are behaviorally unresponsive under anesthesia.

---

## 7. Calibration Constraint

> **Behavioral unresponsiveness under anesthesia is not equivalent to established absence of consciousness.**

And:

> **A valid anesthetic negative control requires convergent, state-specific evidence rather than responsiveness alone.**
