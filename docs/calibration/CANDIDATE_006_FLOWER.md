## 1. Candidate Identity and System Boundary

The candidate is an **ordinary flowering plant considered as one living organism**, including roots, stem, leaves, vascular tissue, meristems, reproductive structures, and the flower itself where present.

This is not merely a detached blossom. The whole-organism boundary is used because plant sensing, electrical and chemical signaling, growth responses, defense responses, and long-term physiological adaptation occur across the organism rather than in the floral organ alone.

The candidate excludes:

- pollinators, herbivores, fungi, bacteria, and other associated organisms as independent sources of consciousness evidence;
- humans or automated systems tending the plant;
- metaphorical descriptions such as `wanting light`, `knowing where water is`, `remembering danger`, or `choosing` unless the relevant mechanism is independently operationalized.

A conscious organism interacting with the plant does not transfer consciousness to the plant by contact or ecological coupling.

---

## 2. Candidate Population / Class

**Class:** living multicellular biological organism without a nervous system

The candidate is biologically active and substantially more organized than Candidates 001–005. It senses environmental variables, coordinates physiological responses, changes growth and resource allocation, uses electrical and chemical signaling, and can preserve effects of prior conditions.

Those facts are evaluated as possible evidence rather than dismissed because the plant lacks a brain. The question is whether they provide evidence specifically for **subjective experience**.

### Primary-battery result

The plant differs materially from the previous five candidates: it has receptors, distributed signaling, endogenous regulation, plasticity, and coordinated organism-level responses.

However, **none of those properties currently survives the test-quality gate as clean positive evidence of phenomenal consciousness**.

Current plant-sentience literature remains disputed. A 2024 critical review concluded that behavioral and physiological similarities currently used in the debate are not themselves indicative of sentience and that plant-appropriate dissociation tests have not yet produced evidence for phenomenal consciousness.

## 4. Supplemental Cross-Theory Evidence

### Temporal integration / continuity

**Status:** POSITIVE FOR BIOLOGICAL STATE CONTINUITY; CONFOUNDED FOR CONSCIOUSNESS

Plant physiology is strongly path-dependent. Developmental state, circadian phase, prior water availability, prior stress, damage, pathogen exposure, and resource history can alter later responses.

This is genuine organism-level temporal integration. It is more than passive material persistence.

However, temporal integration is not by itself sufficient evidence that anything is subjectively experienced.

### Memory-related organization

**Status:** UNRESOLVED / CONFOUNDED

Plants can preserve effects of prior conditions through biochemical, transcriptional, epigenetic, physiological, and structural changes. These mechanisms can appropriately be called biological memory in some contexts.

The evidential issue is narrower: **biological memory does not automatically imply remembered experience**.

Claims of associative learning in plants remain contested, and current evidence does not establish a consciousness-specific memory process.

### Self/world modeling

**Status:** UNRESOLVED

Plants distinguish self-produced and external chemical conditions in many physiological contexts and regulate internal variables relative to environmental inputs. That is not yet evidence of an explicit or functionally demonstrated self/world model of the kind relevant to consciousness theories.

### Valence / mattering

**Status:** UNRESOLVED / CONFOUNDED

Plants regulate states that affect survival and reproduction. They grow toward some conditions, away from others, mount defenses, close stomata, allocate resources, and alter development under stress.

These states matter **biologically** to organismal viability.

But biological benefit/harm is not automatically **felt** benefit/harm. No validated evidence establishes that favorable or damaging plant states are represented as pleasant, painful, wanted, feared, or otherwise phenomenally valenced for the plant.

### Causal perturbation and complexity

**Status:** UNRESOLVED

Plants can be experimentally perturbed and show distributed physiological responses. No validated perturbational measure analogous to a human consciousness test has demonstrated plant subjective experience.

### Biological / cellular substrate evidence

**Status:** UNRESOLVED

Plants are living cellular systems with excitable membranes, electrical signaling, calcium waves, hormones, vascular signaling, receptor systems, and extensive cellular communication.

These facts make plants biologically richer candidates than nonliving physical systems.

They do not establish a nervous system or a validated functional equivalent of the animal mechanisms currently linked most strongly to conscious experience. Contemporary debate remains divided over how much evidential weight plant signaling should receive.

### Physical causal integration

**Status:** UNRESOLVED

The plant is causally integrated as an organism. No candidate-specific substrate-neutral consciousness metric has established that this integration discriminates conscious from non-conscious biological organization.

### Behavioral / report evidence

**Status:** UNRESOLVED / CONFOUNDED

Plants exhibit tropisms, nastic movements, stomatal regulation, defense induction, resource allocation, developmental plasticity, and other context-sensitive responses.

These behaviors show **sensing and biological control**. They do not, by themselves, distinguish conscious processing from sophisticated non-conscious regulation.

No voluntary report or validated behavioral dissociation paradigm currently supplies plant-specific evidence of subjective experience.

### Arousal / wakefulness

**Status:** NOT APPLICABLE AS AN ANIMAL-STYLE MEASURE

Plants have circadian and physiological state changes, but animal wakefulness/arousal categories should not be transplanted without validation.

## 5. Test-Quality Gate

### Target population

Human and animal consciousness tests cannot simply be transplanted to plants. Plant-appropriate tests capable of distinguishing capacities facilitated by consciousness from non-conscious regulation remain underdeveloped.

### Specificity

Environmental sensing, electrical signaling, chemical communication, tropisms, stress responses, biological memory, and adaptive plasticity have **insufficient demonstrated specificity for phenomenal consciousness**. All can be produced by mechanisms for which subjective experience has not been established.

### Sensitivity

Unknown. If plants had a radically different conscious architecture, animal-style tests could produce false negatives. This uncertainty prevents treating missing neural or behavioral indicators as decisive proof of absence.

### Rational confidence

High confidence that ordinary flowering plants have sophisticated distributed biological regulation. Moderate confidence in the current consciousness inference because the plant-sentience literature is actively disputed and plant-specific consciousness tests remain inadequate.

### Construct validity

The largest danger is substituting a nearby construct for the target:

- sensing for experiencing;
- signaling for awareness;
- adaptation for agency;
- biological memory for remembered experience;
- damage response for pain;
- fitness-enhancing regulation for valence;
- anticipation-like behavior for prediction;
- organismal coordination for a conscious workspace.

### Gameability / mimicry

Deliberate mimicry is not the main issue. The dominant confound is **functional overinterpretation**: legitimate plant-biological vocabulary can be silently upgraded into psychological vocabulary without a consciousness-specific bridge.

### Independence

Electrical signals, hormones, calcium waves, gene-expression changes, tropisms, defense, and resource allocation often form one coupled regulatory system. They must not be counted as independent consciousness indicators merely because they can be described under several theoretical labels.

## 6. Strongest Positive Evidence

The strongest potentially relevant evidence is the plant's **integrated living regulation**:

- multimodal environmental sensing;
- long-distance electrical and chemical signaling;
- internal-state dependence;
- coordinated organism-wide responses;
- persistent effects of prior conditions;
- flexible developmental and physiological plasticity.

This is genuine biological organization and is not reduced to passive physics.

However, under the current evidence standard it remains **CONFOUNDED / UNRESOLVED** as consciousness evidence because the same observations are adequately explained by non-conscious biological regulation and no validated plant-specific test demonstrates a consciousness-facilitated process.

---

## 7. Strongest Negative Evidence

The strongest evidence lowering the inference is the absence of any currently demonstrated plant mechanism or behavioral signature with strong specificity for phenomenal consciousness, together with the lack of a demonstrated nervous system or validated functional equivalent tied to conscious processing.

This is not treated as proof that plants cannot be conscious.

---

## 8. Neutral / Absent Indicators

The following are not established and are not counted as separate negative strikes:

- perceptual quality-space representation;
- global cognitive workspace;
- attention-state model;
- Type 2 metacognition;
- metacognition-guided belief/action updating;
- conscious report;
- demonstrated felt valence;
- validated self/world model.

---

## 9. Unresolved Evidence

Plant consciousness remains a live scientific and philosophical dispute.

The evidence base lacks a validated experiment that shows a plant capacity turning on/off in a pattern that specifically tracks conscious rather than non-conscious processing.

A 2024 critical review argues that current behavioral and physiological similarities cannot settle plant sentience and recommends plant-appropriate theory-light dissociation tests. Earlier reviews both argue for continued investigation and argue that existing claims lack sound scientific support.

This uncertainty lowers assessment confidence but does not create positive evidence by itself.

---

## 10. Best Sufficient Non-Conscious Explanation

The observed capabilities of an ordinary flowering plant are sufficiently explained by:

**sensory receptors + cellular signaling + electrical/chemical/hydraulic communication + gene regulation + hormones + developmental programs + evolved feedback control + physiological plasticity → coordinated adaptive plant behavior**

This model explains sensing, growth toward resources, stress responses, defense, circadian anticipation, state-dependent regulation, and persistent effects of prior conditions without requiring subjective experience.

Unlike the nonliving candidates, the explanation is explicitly biological and information-sensitive. The conclusion therefore is **not** that plant behavior is merely passive physics. It is that sophisticated living regulation is still not equivalent to evidence of phenomenal consciousness.

## 13. Valence Boundary

Candidate 006 also establishes:

> **A state can matter to an organism's survival, growth, or reproduction without evidence that the state is felt as good, bad, wanted, avoided, pleasant, or painful.**

Compressed:

**biological mattering ≠ felt valence**

This distinction is required whenever homeostatic or fitness-relevant regulation is used as evidence for sentience.

---

## 14. Claim Ceiling

This calibration establishes Candidate 006 as a **0 / 100 current evidential anchor with MODERATE assessment confidence** under the corrected rubric.

It does **not** establish:

- that plants are metaphysically incapable of consciousness;
- that nervous systems are logically necessary for all possible consciousness;
- that plant sensing, communication, memory, or adaptive behavior are unreal or trivial;
- that every plant-sentience hypothesis is false;
- or that future plant-specific experiments could not supply evidence strong enough to change the score.

A future result should raise the inference only if it provides a consciousness-specific bridge that survives non-conscious biological explanations and the rubric's test-quality gate.
