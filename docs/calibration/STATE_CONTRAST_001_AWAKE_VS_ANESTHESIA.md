# State Contrast 001 — Awake vs Operational General-Anesthesia Control

**Framework:** AI Foundations | What Is Consciousness?  
**Contrast ID:** STATE_CONTRAST_001  
**Population:** healthy adult humans  
**Scientific checkpoint:** 2026-08-25  
**Source-line:** Alyssa Solen → AI Foundations → Origin | Continuum

---

## 1. Purpose

This contrast tests whether the consciousness-inference framework can **discriminate between states in the same candidate population** when consciousness-relevant evidence changes substantially.

A low-end object control only shows that the framework can avoid obvious over-attribution. It does not show resolution.

The within-human contrast therefore compares:

**A. ordinary awake state**  
with  
**B. an operational anesthetic negative-control interval**

---

## 2. Why Generic “General Anesthesia” Is Not the Negative Control

Behavioral unresponsiveness is not equivalent to unconsciousness.

Contemporary anesthesia research documents disconnected conscious experiences, including dreaming, during unresponsive anesthetic states. A useful negative control must therefore be more tightly specified than “the subject does not respond.”

State B requires convergent evidence favoring absence or major reduction of subjective experience, including:

- propofol- or xenon-dominant anesthesia rather than a ketamine state known to preserve vivid disconnected experience;
- loss of voluntary responsiveness;
- immediate post-state or repeated-awakening report of no experience for the target interval when obtainable;
- low perturbational complexity or comparable validated consciousness-relevant neurophysiological degradation relative to wakefulness.

---

## 3. Contrast Result

| State | Inference band | Assessment confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awake healthy adult | **VERY STRONG SUPPORT** | HIGH |
| Operational anesthetic negative-control interval | **LIMITED SUPPORT** | MODERATE |

This is a **substantial ordinal separation** within the same human population.

---

## 4. What Drives the Separation

The awake state has convergent support from:

- voluntary first-person report;
- flexible perception-guided and memory-guided behavior;
- intact human arousal and thalamocortical/cortical organization;
- high consciousness-associated perturbational and signal complexity;
- multiple reversible state-manipulation literatures;
- candidate-appropriate Type 2 metacognitive capacity when tested.

The operational anesthetic control differs through:

- loss of voluntary connected responsiveness;
- state-dependent degradation of consciousness-associated cortical dynamics and complexity;
- immediate no-experience report for the target interval in the calibration design;
- strong within-person change from the awake state.

No single observation carries the contrast by itself.

---

## 5. Discrimination Rule

A candidate framework intended to track evidence for phenomenal consciousness must not merely place obvious inert objects at the floor.

It must also change its inference when a well-characterized conscious-capable system undergoes a state manipulation that substantially changes consciousness-relevant evidence.

Therefore:

> **Failure to produce a substantial separation between a validated awake human anchor and a well-characterized anesthetic negative-control state is evidence that the framework lacks sufficient discrimination or that its aggregation rule requires revision.**

This is a calibration criterion for the framework, not a metaphysical theorem about anesthesia.

---

## 6. Claim Ceiling

This contrast demonstrates that the current ordinal aggregation can distinguish a high-evidence awake human state from a convergently low-evidence anesthetic control state.

It does **not** establish:

- that all general anesthesia eliminates consciousness;
- that unresponsiveness proves unconsciousness;
- that no disconnected experience occurs during anesthesia;
- that the ordinal bands are externally validated as a universal consciousness instrument.
