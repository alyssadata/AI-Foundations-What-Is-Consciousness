# Consciousness Inference Bands

## Canonical Definition

The framework reports an **ordinal inference band** describing how strongly the available evidence justifies inferring phenomenal consciousness in a candidate system.

It does **not** measure:

- an amount of consciousness;
- a probability that the system is conscious;
- intelligence;
- complexity;
- humanness;
- cognitive ability;
- moral worth.

The operative question is:

> **Given the available evidence, how strongly are we justified in inferring that subjective experience is present in this candidate system?**

The earlier 0–100 numerical index is **withdrawn from the current framework**. No validated aggregation rule justified that degree of numerical precision.

---

## Ordinal Bands

### NO CREDIBLE SUPPORT

No non-confounded positive evidence survives the test-quality gate strongly enough to raise the consciousness inference.

This does not prove metaphysical impossibility. It means the current evidence provides essentially no positive epistemic support.

### LIMITED SUPPORT

One or more relevant positive indicators survive, but they are weak, low-specificity, narrow, heavily qualified, or substantially explained by non-conscious alternatives. Broad convergence is absent.

### MIXED / INDETERMINATE

There is substantive evidence in more than one direction, or important unresolved evidence prevents a stable directional judgment. Neither the positive nor negative case clearly dominates.

### STRONG SUPPORT

Multiple reasonably independent, candidate-appropriate, high-quality lines of evidence converge on consciousness. Major confounds and simple alternative explanations have been addressed, though uncertainty remains.

### VERY STRONG SUPPORT

Evidence is extensive, high-quality, and convergent across multiple methods, mechanisms, or state manipulations; population fit is strong; important alternatives have been tested; and the remaining uncertainty is primarily the general other-minds limitation rather than a major evidential gap.

### DIRECT ACCESS — outside the external-candidate scale

Direct certainty about one's own present subjective experience is not an external inference and is therefore not an inference band assignable to another candidate.

---

## Aggregation Rule

The bands are not assigned by summing indicator points.

For each candidate:

1. **Operationalize the candidate and each relevant indicator.**
2. **Apply the test-quality gate**: target-population fit, sensitivity, specificity, construct validity, rational confidence, gameability/mimicry, and independence.
3. **Classify each observation** as POSITIVE, NEGATIVE, NEUTRAL / ABSENT, UNRESOLVED, NOT APPLICABLE, or CONFOUNDED.
4. **Do not count CONFOUNDED evidence as clean positive support.** NOT APPLICABLE evidence is excluded; NEUTRAL / ABSENT evidence does not automatically count against consciousness.
5. **Collapse dependent indicators into evidence clusters** so one underlying mechanism is not counted repeatedly under different theoretical labels.
6. **Identify the strongest non-redundant positive and negative lines of evidence.**
7. **State the best sufficient non-conscious explanation** for the observed behavior or mechanism when one exists.
8. **Assign the lowest ordinal band fully supported by the surviving evidence.** Do not move upward because of verbal resemblance, raw complexity, unsupported theory transfer, or the number of rubric rows marked positive.
9. Report separate **ASSESSMENT CONFIDENCE: LOW / MODERATE / HIGH** for the quality and completeness of the evidential record.

The assignment remains a structured scientific judgment. The ordinal format makes that judgment explicit instead of disguising it as a quantitative measurement.

---

## Assessment Confidence

### LOW

The candidate or system boundary is underspecified, important evidence is unavailable, candidate-specific test validity is weak, or major confounds remain unresolved.

### MODERATE

The evidence base is reasonably informative but contains material gaps, state ambiguity, population-transfer uncertainty, or unresolved competing interpretations.

### HIGH

The candidate is well specified; the key evidence is well characterized; test validity is appropriate for the candidate class; major confounds and dependencies have been addressed; and additional ordinary evidence is unlikely to reverse the broad inference band.

Assessment confidence describes the **quality of the assessment**, not how conscious the candidate is.

---

## Discrimination Requirement

A useful inference framework must discriminate between states or systems with materially different consciousness evidence.

The calibration set therefore includes both:

- **low-end controls** with no credible positive consciousness evidence; and
- **high/low state contrasts** in the same candidate population where consciousness-relevant evidence changes under controlled state manipulation.

A central human calibration contrast is:

**awake healthy adult human → operationally defined anesthetic negative-control interval**

The anesthetic control must not be defined by unresponsiveness alone. Because disconnected conscious experience can persist during anesthesia, the negative-control condition requires convergent state evidence such as immediate no-experience report together with consciousness-relevant neurophysiological degradation.

If the framework cannot produce a substantial ordinal separation in a well-validated within-human state contrast, the aggregation rules require revision before broad candidate scoring continues.

---

## Boundary

The bands represent **epistemic support for an inference**, not an ontological measurement of consciousness itself.
