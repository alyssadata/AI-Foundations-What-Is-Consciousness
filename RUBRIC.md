## 1. Target Construct

This rubric evaluates the **degree of epistemic justification for inferring phenomenal consciousness in a candidate system**.

Phenomenal consciousness means subjective experience: there is something it is like to be the system at that time.

The rubric does **not** treat cognition, intelligence, selection, report, attention, metacognition, agency, memory, or complexity as equivalent to consciousness.

### Awareness boundary

`Awareness` is not scored as an independent indicator because the term is ambiguous and can become circular.

If `awareness` means subjective awareness, it simply renames the target phenomenon.

If it means functional access to information, it must be operationalized using measurable properties such as:

- selective attention;
- global availability;
- metacognitive monitoring;
- attention-state modeling;
- self/world modeling;
- or behavior produced from accessed information.

**functional access ≠ phenomenal consciousness**

## 2. Evidence Statuses

Every indicator is assigned one of the following statuses for a candidate:

- **POSITIVE** — evidence raises the consciousness inference under at least one scientifically credible account.
- **NEGATIVE** — evidence lowers the consciousness inference because a relevant predicted property is demonstrably absent, an anti-indicator is present, or intervention evidence conflicts with a consciousness-supporting account.
- **NEUTRAL / ABSENT** — no positive evidence is present, but the absence is not itself justified as evidence against consciousness.
- **UNRESOLVED** — available evidence is insufficient, disputed, inaccessible, or depends on an unsettled system boundary.
- **NOT APPLICABLE** — the indicator or test is not meaningful for this candidate class.
- **CONFOUNDED** — an apparent indicator is present, but a known alternative explanation, cue, imitation pathway, or gameable surface behavior prevents it from being treated as clean evidence.

### Negative-evidence rule

**Missing a positive indicator is not automatically negative evidence.**

Absence counts against consciousness only when there is a defensible reason to expect the indicator in conscious members of the relevant candidate class, or when a theory treats the property as necessary/sufficiently sensitive and the candidate clearly lacks it.

For example:

- no language is not automatically evidence of unconsciousness;
- no metacognition is not automatically evidence of unconsciousness;
- no valence is not automatically evidence of unconsciousness;
- physical persistence alone is neither positive nor negative evidence.

# 3. Primary Theory-Derived Indicator Battery

The primary battery follows the current theory-derived-indicator approach used in contemporary AI-consciousness assessment. The source framework groups fourteen potential indicators under recurrent processing, global workspace, computational higher-order theories, attention schema theory, predictive processing, agency, and embodiment.

These are used as **evidential indicators**, not as individually necessary conditions and not as a jointly sufficient checklist.

## Recurrent Processing

### RPT-1 — Algorithmic recurrence

Does relevant information re-enter earlier or continuing processing rather than only move through a one-way feedforward chain?

### RPT-2 — Organized integrated perceptual representation

Does the system form organized representations in which features are integrated into structured perceptual wholes rather than remaining isolated feature detections?

## Global Workspace

### GWT-1 — Specialized parallel subsystems

Are there multiple specialized processes capable of operating in parallel?

### GWT-2 — Limited-capacity workspace with selection

Is there a capacity-limited information bottleneck or workspace with a mechanism that selectively admits some information rather than everything equally?

### GWT-3 — Global availability / broadcast

Can selected information become broadly available to otherwise distinct subsystems for flexible downstream use?

### GWT-4 — State-dependent attentional control

Can the system use its current state to direct or sequence access to different information sources in order to perform complex tasks?

## Computational Higher-Order Accounts

### HOT-1 — Generative or top-down perceptual processing

Can perceptual processing be influenced by internally generated, top-down, predictive, or noise-sensitive activity rather than being purely stimulus-driven?

### HOT-2 — Metacognitive monitoring

Does the system monitor the reliability, uncertainty, source, or quality of its own representations?

### HOT-3 — Metacognition-guided belief/action updating

Does metacognitive monitoring causally influence broader belief formation, decision making, or action selection?

### HOT-4 — Structured quality-space coding

Does the system represent perceptual qualities in a sparse, smooth, structured representational space rather than only as arbitrary discrete labels?

## Attention Schema

### AST-1 — Model of attention

Does the system maintain a predictive representation of its own current attention state that can be used to control attention?

## Predictive Processing

### PP-1 — Predictive coding

Does the system generate predictions and use prediction error or related feedback to update lower-level processing?

## Agency and Embodiment

### AE-1 — Minimal flexible agency

Does the system learn from feedback and select outputs in pursuit of goals, especially when goals compete or circumstances change?

### AE-2 — Output-input contingency modeling

Does the system model how its own outputs alter subsequent inputs and use that model in perception or control?

## 4. Dependency / Double-Counting Rule

The primary indicators are **not statistically or conceptually independent**.

Examples:

- some global-workspace properties presuppose recurrence;
- predictive coding includes recurrent/top-down structure;
- higher-order indicators build on one another;
- embodiment often implies some form of agency.

Therefore:

**Do not assign equal points to indicators and sum them.**

When two indicators are supported by the same underlying mechanism, record the dependency and do not treat them as two independent lines of evidence.

Convergence is strongest when evidence comes from **genuinely different mechanisms, methods, theories, or interventions**.

# 5. Supplemental Cross-Theory Evidence

The fourteen-indicator battery does not exhaust current consciousness science. The following dimensions are recorded separately so the framework can assess biological, artificial, simple physical, and mixed candidate systems without pretending one theory is settled.

## Temporal integration / continuity

Does information or internal state persist across time in a way that causally constrains later processing?

Physical persistence alone does not qualify.

## Memory-related organization

Does memory contribute to the construction, maintenance, reconstruction, or temporal organization of current experience-relevant processing?

Memory is evidence only in the relevant functional context; storage alone is not consciousness evidence.

## Self/world modeling

Does the system represent a distinction between itself, its own state/actions, and its environment?

A self-model is not required by every theory and is not sufficient for consciousness.

## Valence / mattering

Is there evidence that states are represented or regulated as better/worse, rewarding/aversive, beneficial/harmful, wanted/avoided, or otherwise significant for the system?

Valence may strengthen some consciousness inferences, especially concerning sentience or welfare, but **absence of valence is not automatically evidence of absence of phenomenal consciousness**.

## Causal perturbation and complexity

When a candidate's internal system is perturbed, does the response show both integration and differentiation in a way associated with conscious states?

For human brains, TMS-EEG measures such as the Perturbational Complexity Index are relevant here. This evidence is population-specific and must not be transplanted uncritically to unrelated systems.

## Biological / cellular substrate evidence

When the candidate is biological, record relevant neural, cellular, dendritic, thalamocortical, or other biological organization associated with contemporary theories.

This allows biological-substrate and emerging dendritic-integration accounts to be represented rather than silently assuming computational functionalism.

## Physical causal integration

Where meaningful, assess whether the system has internally integrated causal structure of the kind emphasized by Integrated Information Theory or related causal-structure accounts.

Ordinary physical connectedness is not enough; the relevant claim concerns the organization of causal relations within the system.

## Behavioral / report evidence

Record voluntary report, command following, discrimination, learning, flexible behavior, pain-related behavior, and other externally observable evidence.

Behavioral evidence is interpreted relative to the candidate population. A behavior that is highly specific for consciousness in one population may be easily generated without consciousness in another.

## Arousal / wakefulness — biological candidates only

For human and animal candidates, arousal and wakefulness can affect the capacity to express consciousness and the interpretation of behavioral tests.

Arousal is neither equivalent to nor sufficient for consciousness.

# 6. Test-Quality Gate

Before any observation changes the score, record the quality of the test or evidence used.

For each important item ask:

### Target population

Is this measure actually validated or theoretically meaningful for this type of candidate?

### Specificity

If the indicator is present, how often could a non-conscious candidate also produce it?

### Sensitivity

If the candidate were conscious, how likely is this indicator to be present or detected?

### Rational confidence

How well justified are the claimed sensitivity, specificity, and theoretical connection to consciousness?

### Construct validity

Does the measure target phenomenal consciousness, or only a related capacity such as reportability, attention, cognition, memory, or task compliance?

### Gameability / mimicry

Can the apparent indicator be deliberately engineered, copied, cued, scripted, or behaviorally mimicked without implementing the mechanism that gives the indicator evidential force?

### Independence

Is this genuinely new evidence, or the same underlying mechanism counted again under another label?

# 7. Population-Specific Evidence Modules

The same core epistemic target is used across all candidates, but **not every test is universal**.

## Human / clinical module

Relevant evidence can include:

- repeated standardized behavioral assessment;
- voluntary command following;
- covert command following using EEG or fMRI;
- resting-state and task-based neurophysiology;
- TMS-EEG / perturbational complexity;
- effects of anesthesia, lesions, stimulation, or recovery;
- arousal and confound assessment.

## Nonhuman animal module

Relevant evidence can include:

- homologous neurobiology;
- flexible learning and action;
- nociception and pain-related behavior;
- sensory integration;
- attention and memory;
- sleep/wake organization;
- species-appropriate perturbation or neural evidence.

## AI / computational module

Relevant evidence can include:

- architecture inspection;
- mechanistic interpretability;
- causal intervention / ablation;
- state and memory analysis;
- controlled behavioral experiments;
- system-boundary specification;
- evidence that apparent self-report or introspection tracks internal mechanisms rather than prompting or imitation.

## Simple physical / engineered object module

For objects such as blankets, bulbs, watches, scales, printers, planets, or stars, do not infer consciousness merely from:

- physical persistence;
- state change;
- energy use;
- feedback in the ordinary engineering sense;
- complexity;
- responsiveness to external forces;
- or the fact that components interact.

The relevant question remains whether there is credible evidence connecting the candidate to mechanisms or indicators that support an inference of subjective experience.

# 8. Scoring

The repository retains the 0–100 **consciousness-inference score**.

It measures:

> **degree of epistemic justification for inferring consciousness in the candidate, given the available evidence**

It is not an amount of consciousness and is not produced by mechanically summing indicator points.

### Score anchors

- **0** — essentially no credible evidence supporting the inference
- **25** — weak evidence; non-conscious accounts are much better supported
- **50** — genuinely ambiguous overall evidence
- **75** — strong converging evidence
- **95+** — extremely strong inference from multiple independent, high-quality lines of evidence
- **100** — direct certainty; reserved for one's own present subjective experience, not an externally observed candidate

## Separate assessment-confidence rating

Every score must also report:

**ASSESSMENT CONFIDENCE: LOW / MODERATE / HIGH**

This rating describes confidence in the *quality and completeness of the evidence used to assign the score*, not confidence that the candidate is conscious.

Examples:

- `Score 5 / 100 — Assessment confidence HIGH` can mean the candidate is well understood and provides almost no consciousness evidence.
- `Score 5 / 100 — Assessment confidence LOW` can mean very little is known, so the low score is fragile.

# 9. Required Candidate Record

Before a score is preserved, record:

1. **Candidate identity and system boundary** — exactly what is being evaluated.
2. **Candidate population/class** — physical object, engineered system, AI, animal, human, etc.
3. **Primary indicator battery** — status and evidence for each applicable indicator.
4. **Supplemental evidence** — temporal, valence, biological, causal, behavioral, or other relevant evidence.
5. **Test-quality notes** — specificity, sensitivity, rational confidence, construct validity, gameability, independence.
6. **Strongest positive evidence.**
7. **Strongest negative evidence.**
8. **Neutral/absent indicators that must not be mistaken for negative evidence.**
9. **Unresolved evidence and unavailable measurements.**
10. **Best sufficient non-conscious explanation** of the observed behavior, when one exists. This explanation is an alternative model, not automatic proof of non-consciousness.
11. **Consciousness-inference score: 0–100.**
12. **Assessment confidence: LOW / MODERATE / HIGH.**
13. **Claim ceiling** — what the result does and does not establish.
14. **New calibration constraint**, if one is earned.
